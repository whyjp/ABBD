# Blueprint drawing rules

A person should understand and judge an architecture from `.abbd/blueprint/index.html` quickly. What makes a blueprint hard to read is not the number of relations. It is lines that run the same way but are drawn apart, lines that run against the eye or out of their way, and views that try to show too much at once. The bar is a diagram a person polished by hand in a tool like draw.io, not a dump of extracted edges. So the blueprint follows three principles, and every rule below comes from one of them.

- **Decomposition.** The blueprint is a tree of views, all drawn the same way. The overview shows the top level. Every unit that holds others has a view of its children, with that unit's contracts on the frame. A box that holds units opens its view. Each view stays small because the state is decomposed, never because relations are left out.
- **Convergence.** Lines that come from one place travel together as one trunk and branch only near their ends. Lines that go to one place, or to boxes side by side that the same places reach, merge midway into one trunk and split only in the gap above them. A trunk carries the word all its lines say, or a count from three lines. A reader follows one trunk instead of a braid.
- **Flow.** A drawing reads top to bottom and left to right. What calls or uses sits above and to the left. What it calls or uses sits below and to the right. Lines point down or right, and run on the grid in the spaces between boxes, the short way. The few that must climb are drawn as any other line of their kind: which way a line runs along the layout is the layout's doing, not a meaning, and a cycle is two lines that point at each other. Its word and head say what a line means.

`internal/screen/legible.go` checks every rule on the rendered SVG, which is what a reader sees. `internal/screen/trace.go` traces every relation along the drawn ink, the way a reader follows a line, for rules 16 to 23. `internal/screen/fixtures_test.go` applies the checks to every view of the real states under `internal/screen/testdata`. The rule numbers below match the checker's messages. `abbd lint` holds every drawing to the Drawing criteria below, which build on the same measures.

## Decomposition

1. **At most 16 boxes per view.** Top-level contracts don't count, because they are drawn as chips, and neither does a focus view's twin. A level that holds more is split only along a grouping the project shows (see Grouping levels). Otherwise it stays as it is, a debt of this rule, and its focus views read it one box at a time.
2. **No relation is dropped.** Every live relation between present units appears in some view. It can be a line, a bundle, a contract chip, the list of a reader's couplings under a module's inside, or the list of relations between two contracts. Each wire and chip records the stored relations it stands for, and the test compares those records with the state.
3. **Width budget.** Every drawing is at most 1320px wide. A 1600px desktop window shows that much beside the page's nav. The nav is 13rem wide, the page has a 10px gutter and a section 10px of padding, so a drawing's box is 1333px wide in that window. A 1320px budget would scroll sideways under a 15rem nav and 16px and 14px of padding. A drawing wider than its box, as in a 1280px window, scrolls inside its box. A unit's page sets its text in a panel below its drawing, not in a column beside it, so a unit's view takes the same width as the overview. A layer that is too wide wraps into rows. A module on the overview whose chips need more than 480px wraps them into up to two rows inside its box instead of widening, so lines can pass beside it; the last row folds what still does not fit into one `+N` chip that names them all. A frame never grows past the budget: chips that do not fit on their side move to the other side, and those that fit on neither fold into one `+N` chip. On a 390px screen the drawing scrolls inside its box and the page itself never scrolls sideways.

## Focus views

Some views stay a tangle however they are laid out: their boxes use each other many times over. A reader cannot follow one box's lines through all the others. So such a view is also read one box at a time, through views derived from it (`focus.go`).

A view is dense when it draws more than two relations per box on average. Each box of a dense view then gets a focus view. A focus view of box X in view V is a partial graph of V:

- **Selected.** X and the boxes of V that X uses or that use X: the ring.
- **Drawn.** Only the lines with an end at X. A line between two ring boxes is left out here and drawn in the focus view of either of them.
- **Lifted.** X's lines to anything outside V's scope stay on the frame, lifted to the largest unit that holds their end, exactly as in V. On the overview, a contract X provides brings the modules that use it, and a contract X uses brings the modules that provide it.
- **Collapsed.** Nothing new. A ring box that holds units stays one box, as in V.
- **Placed.** Callers above X, what X uses below it, and X at the middle of the drawing. The lines from the frame to X run down the same middle, so they drop straight into it, and each row of the ring packs around them. A row that cannot reach that far without passing the width budget moves in only as far as it must.
- **Mental map.** The boxes of each ring row stand left to right in the order they stand in V, so a box the reader found left of another in V is still left of it here. A ring too wide for one row splits into rows that each take every k-th box: each row still runs across V from left to right, and a box of one row stands over a gap in the next, where its line passes down. A test holds the order and the centred focus across the fixtures.
- **Both ways.** In the focus views of an inside, a ring box that both uses X and is used by it is drawn once. Its two lines are a mutual pair, folded into one line with a head at each end. Where its only lines are that pair and X meets at most one other such box, it stands beside X in X's row, the folded line straight across between them, so X sits between its two partners. Any view drawn by choosing among tactics tries the same move, for any box whose only lines are such a pair with one other box. The view also tries the folded lines of X as a fan (rule 18), and such boxes seated in the ring row next to X where that row wraps, so a pair that reads both ways stands in rows next to each other (P1). The view keeps such a drawing only where it reads no worse on every total the gate holds it to. Where it does not, as about a hub that many boxes use both ways, the box is still drawn twice, once above among X's callers and once below among what X uses. The second box is a twin, a unit of the drawing only; its tooltip and sub-line say it is also drawn above, and rule 1 counts it once. The focus views of the overview still draw every such box twice. That twin is a known debt: it goes once opposite lines at a hub keep a two-lane road (rule 4).
- **Width.** A focus view takes the width budget every view takes, and its unit boxes show only the last segment of a path-like name, as frame ports do.
- **Back to the stored graph.** Every line keeps the stored relations it stands for, as in V. Each ring box opens its own focus view, X opens its inside when it has one, and the section links back to V.

A focus view stores nothing: it is cut from V's own lines at render time, never found again, so it draws no line, chip or box that V lacks, except the static lines V leaves out at rest and lists under its picture (below). V stays as it is, and every relation V draws has a box at one end, so it is drawn in that box's focus view too. A test holds both directions across the fixtures. A reader who only wants to follow one box need not leave V. A line's ink is drawn in pieces: its own stub, the buses it rides, and their drops. So each line also gets a route, one path along its whole way from the shape it leaves to the shape it reaches. The route is drawn unseen above all the ink and takes no pointer, so the pointer always rests on ink and the ink keeps its title, which names every line it carries. Each piece of ink names every route that runs along it, so pointing at a stub, a bus or a drop shows each of those lines as its route, whole and in its own colour, with the line's word, count and origin marks, while every other piece of ink fades and only the shapes at those lines' ends stay lit. While the pointer rests on a box or a frame chip, or a box that is a link has the keyboard's focus, all the ink and every dot fades, and exactly the box's own relations show: each line in or out with an end there, as its route, and the shapes at their other ends. A box colours every one of those routes its own colour, whatever the line's kind, which its dash and arrowhead still show. A chip wears no unit's colour, so it leaves each route the line's own, and a contract chip on a box dims and lights with its box. Every box and chip no relation of the view joins to it fades too (`hover.go`). A route's arrowheads paint in the route's colour, set on the route, so no engine draws them black. The page holds no script, so this is plain CSS: one rule per shape number and one per route number, and each route carries, as inline variables, the colour each of its end boxes gives it. `hover_line_test.go` points at every piece of ink and every shape of every fixture. It holds that ink lights exactly the routes that run along it, each whole between its two shapes and lying on its own ink, and that a shape lights exactly its one-hop neighbours, in one colour, with no lit stroke reaching any other shape. 14 stretches of bus track carry no line's way, between two lines' drops on a shared track. Cutting those tracks back raised the fixtures' weighed sums past their ceilings, so that count may only fall. 40 lines ride a bus past where they leave it and come back along another track, a routing defect hover shows rather than hides, and that count may only fall too. Every drawing takes part however many shapes and routes it holds, and a page carries the rules for its own shapes and routes only, so a page with no shape to point at carries none. A box that opens nothing takes no keyboard focus, so it follows the pointer only. Hover and focus take part only where a pointer can rest, and focus only when it came from the keyboard, so a tap or a click never leaves a view faded. Under a dense view the page also lists every focus view as a row of links, and a box of V that opens nothing else opens its focus view. A focus view of the overview sits on the page of its unit, not on the overview's page (see Pages). The graph file holds no focus view.

A focus view never groups, and neither does any other view. The renderer draws no box the state lacks, so a cycle is drawn in the view of the unit that really holds it: its units keep their own boxes, the lines that close it climb under rule 13, and each of its boxes is read on its own through its focus view.

**A list of the lines the cut leaves out.** A view that draws fewer lines than it has says so under its picture, in one folded list: each undrawn pair of boxes once, under the box that uses the other (the hub, where a hub folds the pair), with a link to that box's focus view, which draws it. No list stands where nothing is left out, and the picture itself carries no mark of it.

**Static lines rest in the list.** A line whose relations all rest on a static dependency, an import, a trait, a macro, a build setting, a link or a convention with no call or data on record, is drawn dotted and named 정적 의존 (import·구현·매크로·빌드 설정으로만 확인됨, 호출 근거 없음). Most of a large repository's lines are such imports, and drawn at rest they bury the lines that carry calls and data. So the overview and each inside leave a static line out at rest and list it in the same folded list, marked 정적 의존, under the box that uses the other, with a link to that box's focus view, which draws every line it has. The overview and an inside decide by pair, two boxes or a box and a chip: a pair is left out only when every line of it is static, so no pair is both drawn and listed. A static pair stays drawn where it reaches a contract, where it joins two stages of the primary flow or two boxes that follow each other on a stored flow, where a reader or a human stated one of its relations rather than an extractor finding it or marked one to be drawn, and, on the overview, where the view's lead or the stored overview record rides it: a reader follows the code along those. Where the lead is the one the lines show, with `inferred_lead: true` and no stored flow, it is found again from the lines a view draws, so there a static pair also stays where it is the last line into a box something uses, or the last line out of a box nothing uses: leaving it out would give the lead another start, or none. A stored flow's lead rides the flow's own pairs, which stay drawn. A line left out still takes part in the layering, toward the other box or the frame, so the boxes keep their rows where the layout can, and on the overview each left-out line keeps one track of room in the gap below the row of its upper box, one for each direction it is stored in, so leaving lines out does not shrink the gap until a line's last stretch before a box is shorter than 30px. The room is an estimate: it does not let two lines share a track. The box's focus view draws the line, so every left-out line is drawn at one level. An inside counts the pairs between its boxes that it leaves out, as the overview does, so its levels, its element count and lint read them. The rule reads the relations' `via` only, the same for every repository. `static_lines: true` in `.abbd/project.yaml` draws every static line at rest again. Whether a static dependency's symbols are used at all is not on record, so the list says the line has no call on record, not that it is unused.

**A matrix beside a dense cyclic view.** A focus view reads a tangle one box at a time, but it does not say which boxes form one cycle or what two boxes both use; a dependency structure matrix answers those at a glance (Sangal et al. 2005), while the drawing still answers what connects to what and in which order (Okoe et al. 2019). So a full view, the overview or a unit's inside, whose boxes are joined densely and round cycles keeps its drawing as it is and adds a table under it, folded until a reader opens it (`matrix.go`). It has a row and a column per box in the order a reader meets them, row by row and left to right, and in each cell how many stored relations the row's box uses the column's by, read from the lines the view draws. A cell whose column's box reaches the row's box back is tinted: the two share a cycle. Which views get one is read against each view's own range, never a fixed count. A view of n boxes that joins them at all joins at least n-1 pairs and at most n(n-1)/2, and it gets the table when its joined pairs stand past a third of the way from the one to the other, it draws four boxes at least, as level L counts lines per box, and at least half its boxes share a cycle of the lines its layout layered (`Cycle`, Pages). Across the 99 full views of four boxes or more that 18 evaluation sets draw, 20 have half their boxes on a cycle, and their density stands at 19% of its range or less in all but five: a four-box ring at exactly a third, which reads plainly as lines, and four views between 40% and 67%, in four repositories. The counts and the density are read from the lines the view draws, so an overview whose density cut lists lines under the picture leaves those out. The table is plain HTML, with its own style, so a page without one is byte for byte what it was, and on a narrow screen it scrolls inside its box.

## Convergence

4. **One bundle per place and direction.** Lines of one kind that leave one edge of a box share a departure bus: one stub out of the box, then one trunk down through every row at least two of its lines still pass, heading row by row toward the places they reach. A line leaves the trunk in the gap above the first row it would pass alone. Lines of one kind that reach one edge of a box share an arrival bus: one trunk through every row they pass, one track in the gap above the box, and a drop into each chip. A line joins that trunk once it is off its departure trunk, so lines from different places merge midway and split only at the end; a line that rides its departure trunk into its last gap steps onto the arrival track where the trunk crosses that gap. Boxes side by side in one row that are reached from exactly the same places share one arrival bus that splits only in the gap above them. Boxes reached from places that only overlap get a bus each: a shared trunk would draw a path from every one of its places to every box it splits to (rule 18). The one exception is a shared bus. Two or more boxes that the same two or more places all reach, by lines of one kind running down, in one row or in several, may share one arrival bus. Its track gathers those places' lines and drops into each of the boxes, so a place that reaches them all does not split in two right below itself. A place whose every line of that kind rides it drops its stub straight onto that track. Every other line into one of those boxes joins the shared bus's drop there, on a track of its own that ends on the drop. Every place on a shared bus reaches every box it feeds, so the bus joins no two places that no line joins (rule 18). Where shared buses are drawn, they take the place of the same-row bus above. The renderer draws such a view both ways, with shared buses and without, and keeps the shared buses only when a reader pays less. It weighs each way on the routed ink. It counts the pairs of lines of one kind where one ends on the other though some place on one never reaches a box on the other, the crossings, how far lines of different bundles run side by side, and the bends and jogs along each line's whole way. The shared buses stay only with no more of the first three and fewer of the last. In a focus view, a line between rows next to each other whose two boxes' spans meet joins no bus and drops straight from one box into the other. Every line there has the focus at one end, so its bus would only gather lines that could each drop straight, and turn each of them twice. Where the box's other lines of that kind fork into that same row anyway, and all of them leave one place, such lines join the fork instead: one stem, one track and a drop into each box, as a person draws it. The stem stands over one of them, which still drops straight, and no stub runs beside the stem through its track. The other branches turn twice, which B1 allows a fork. A hub still takes its lines into it as one bundle (H2). There the other lines on that edge spread to make room between the straight drops, and a bus with no side left there for its `×N` moves past the next pin into the empty room. In every view, a line that is not pinned keeps between the pinned lines on either side of it, with room for the lines between, so keeping the order never pushes a pinned line off its x, such as a chip's line pinned over its chain. Lines that all ride one departure bus, one into each box, get no second bus: they split off the departure track straight into their boxes. Frame ports folded into one `+N` chip are one place, so their lines share the chip's slots and buses. A line between two modules that use each other reads both ways, so two such lines that share a bus join their far ends, which may not relate (rule 18). The renderer draws each of them on buses of its own pair, and keeps the bundled drawing only where drawing them apart runs a line more than rule 15 allows out of its way or climbs more lines than rule 13 allows. The checker counts each of them as a bundle of its own, as it does a straight drop. Each drop shows how many of the bus's lines reach that box as `×N` when they share no word (rule 22). The checker counts lines that leave or reach a box edge beside the bundle of their kind, and expects 0 of each. It groups the lines by their kind alone. A line that drops straight needs no bundle, so the checker leaves it out, and the fixtures still hold 0. Without the straight drops, on the routing before them, the checker grouped by kind finds none. On a spine, the one row a primary flow laid across holds (O4), or in a stage's own row down the flow, a line between two stages that stand next to each other, both ends on the boxes and not on a chip, joins no bus: it runs straight across from one box's side into the facing side of the other, as a pipeline is drawn by hand. Under the row it would leave its box beside the bus of its kind, dip and come back up (B3). Lines between the same two neighbours take lanes 12px apart about the middle of the height the boxes share, so two lines one each way never merge. Two neighbours that use each other get a hop each way, not the one two-headed line a mutual pair takes elsewhere, so the hop in stage order carries one head and the other runs against it. Every other line between two stages of the spine runs in the gap above the spine, from top to top, on its box's buses: under the spine it crossed every line the stages send down to the boxes below, 21 crossings on vector's overview with its five stages marked, against 4 over it once the rows below follow the spine (O4). The checker counts a hop as a bundle of its own, as it does a straight drop, and lines of one dotting that leave or reach one edge at one point as one stub, whatever ink each runs on past it. A dotted line that leaves a solid one's stub still reads as a split. A line that climbs is inked as any other, so it may ride a road with lines that do not where they run the same way and are dotted alike. Where it would overlap such a line running the opposite way, or dotted differently, the overlap counts as a clash, whose total no move may raise. Sharing reroutes the other lines too, and can then set lines that do not climb opposite on one track or off a two-lane road. So the view is drawn both ways, and keeps its climbing lines on roads of their own wherever sharing reads worse on some total the gate holds, as read before colours are painted, or on its crossings. That check, not the clash count, holds the two-lane rule there.

   **Roads.** The buses above bundle lines by the place they leave or reach. Lines from different places that still run side by side were left apart, so a busy view repeated parallel lines across and down, and a reader could tell them apart only by pointing. So the ink is a road and each line a bus route on it. Once a view's lines are laid, a straight run of a line moves onto a parallel run of its style within 12px that it runs beside for 16px or more, and the two are drawn as one road that the lines join and leave where their ways part, with a dot at each split and merge (rule 17). A branch of a fork that turns off its stem at another height than its siblings moves onto their track, and a run before a stair moves onto the run past it, which takes the step out. Where a box between the two runs of a stair keeps either from moving onto the other, the one line may take the stair another way round: its step crosses at another height, and its far run comes down up to 96px further out, clear of every box. On ripgrep's overview the user said ripgrep's line into grep-matcher would read cleanly round the left ("ripgrep -> grep-searcher / grep-matcher 로 가는 선은 좌측으로 우회했다면 깔끔하게 표현되었을 것"): it came down the middle, crossed grep-searcher's crowded row and swung round grep-searcher and back. Now it steps left on the bus above grep-printer's row and comes down left of grep-printer. Such a move often costs nothing on its own but frees a run the line shared with another, so it is kept where a move of a whole run then reads better. A split where lines arrive from two sides is a merge, and the moves no longer count it as a split far from where the lines go, as J1 does not. A line's first and last runs move only along the edge span its end may meet, so drops into one box can merge too. A move is kept only where no line then passes through a box, turns back, folds onto itself or gains a jog, and the view gains no crossing, no turn, no fork height and no split away from the rows it splits toward (J1), and loses one of them or runs less ink beside other ink. Each line keeps its own route, its whole way from its start to its end, unseen at rest and lit whole in its colour while a reader points at a box, a chip or any road it rides. A road may so join places no relation joins, which rule 18 allows for roads. Roads come in two classes. A run that holds a line's first or last run is a local road: only lines of one kind and dotting whose ends there are the same sort of shape, a box or a chip, ride it, so each line leaves and arrives in its own look, with its own head. In the middle a run is a highway, which lines of any kind or dotting ride. Climbing lines and forward ones keep their own roads. A highway piece wears the kind most of its lines have, draws no head, and its ink joins every kind's, so its dots mark where any lines split or merge, and pointing at it lights the lines that ride it. No move leaves a line whose relations arise only in design with less than 30px of ink of its own, the least dress draws dotted. A run that carries both solid and dotted lines may also move without its dotted ones, unless it holds a line's first or last run: a box's stub stays whole, since split it leaves the box as two stubs side by side, which on obs-studio's frontend extensions view cost two detours (B4), two combs and 48px of parallel ink. Once every other move has settled, a last pass counts a fork's heights apart by dotting, so where no move has set a dotted branch on its solid siblings' track, the dotted branch keeps a track of its own while its solid neighbours join a road. Counting them apart from the start held a dotted branch off a track it could share, as on the self fixture's /abbd page, a fork level more. A dotted line the view's lead rides is the one dotted line the roads do not keep in sight: its hop paints it in solid flow ink at rest, so a track of its own beside a solid road would only draw a second solid line there. The renderer reads the lead's hops from the boxes and the lines' ends before it routes, and counts such a line as never hidden. A road several boxes' lines ride rests in one grey, like every line (rule 11). Only the drawing choose keeps, and each focus view's, is drawn on roads, so roads never move a box or change which drawing a view keeps. A road cuts a dotted line's ink into paths at each split and merge, so dress weighs the dotted paths of one kind that meet end to end together against the 30px it draws dotted. No view gained a point where ink passes through ink, and no view grew taller. The segments count rose, since it counts each path's stretches and a road is cut into more paths than the buses were.
5. **At most 4 lines per channel.** Vertical lines closer than 12px count as one channel. A fifth line takes a lane past a wider gap.
6. **Parallel lines stay apart.** Two lines never run closer than 5px to each other for more than 24px, except within 26px of a shared end.
7. **At most 2 crossings per drawn line, counted after bundling.** A drawn line is counted where it arrives: a bus is one line however many pieces draw it. Lines run on a grid, so every crossing is at a right angle.
14. **No braids.** Lines of different bundles may run side by side within one channel for at most 200px per drawn line, summed over every pair, away from their ends. Lines that run that far together belong on one trunk.

The lines that leave one edge of a box and the lines that reach it share that edge's slots, so a line arriving at a box's bottom never lands on a line leaving it there. A line that passes a row never takes the x of a line passing the row next to it, since the two would meet in the gap between the rows and read as one. A line's stub, or its drop, keeps clear of the chains passing the row next to its box edge for the same reason.

Each trunk runs down the chain of one of its lines, its lead. A departure trunk takes the chain of its deepest line nearest the box. An arrival trunk takes the chain of the line that joins it first. The bus's stub or drop stands right over that chain, so the trunk leaves or enters its box without a bend. Only one departure track may meet an arrival trunk, at the trunk's top. Any other line that leaves a departure trunk for that arrival trunk steps down off its departure track onto a short track of its own, which ends on the trunk. That step stands between the two trunks and never past the departure trunk, where its track would hook back. It takes the first x no passing line and no other step uses, two lanes or more off the arrival trunk, then one lane off. Where the trunks stand too close for that, it stands halfway between them. A line that rides its departure trunk into its last gap steps down onto its arrival track at an x that no other step and no passing line uses. It steps down where the trunk ends when it can, and above its own end when the trunk runs on below. Where every x from there to its end is taken, it takes the nearest free x past its end or behind the trunk, up to 32 lanes out. Only when none of those is free does it step where it would first have, on a line already there. A step and a short track also keep clear of the stubs of the boxes above their gap, with shared buses drawn or not. A step keeps clear of a line of its own trunk that drops to its end in that gap, when its arrival track also carries lines from other places. A line that drops from its box straight onto an arrival trunk's top holds that top, so a departure track bound for the trunk steps down onto a short track of its own instead. Each of these keeps a reader on the trunk from turning onto ink that leads to places the trunk's lines never reach (rule 18). The last three came with the wide drawings, where boxes stand in line more often and their stubs met other lines' steps.

Two modules on the overview that depend on each other directly are joined by one line with a head at each end, drawn the way the eye reads, instead of two lines of which one would climb. Its tooltip names both directions and it stands for both relations.

Two boxes in any view that use each other by relations of one kind are a mutual pair: one line from A to B and one from B to A, both ending on the boxes themselves, of one dotting, and no other line between them either way. Drawn apart such a pair reads as one line drawn twice. The router's fold action draws it as one line with a head at each end, in one stroke like any other line, that runs the way the eye reads. The folded line rides no bus or road of a line that reads one way: there its heads would read as arrivals from that line's places, a relation no one stored (rule 18). So its far end has its own stub and its own head, and no head stands on another. In a focus view tried with a fan (`tactic.fan`), folded lines of one kind and dotting that share one end, their hub, may share the hub's slot and roads with each other and with no other line: a fan of lines that each read both ways, read as lines into one box read on a bus, each line's far end with its own head. Pointing at a branch lights that line's two relations only. A fan is kept only where the view reads no worse on every total the gate holds it to. A line that ends on a chip, a pair of two kinds or two dottings, and a pair that shares only one end keep two lanes (#232). The policy folds on the drawing it keeps: it makes that drawing's moves again after the fold, so the lines the fold does not touch keep the modes they were drawn in. The chooser and the focus views keep the folded drawing only where it reads no worse on every total the gate holds a view to, on the crossings and on the phantom pairs. Failing that, they try folding only the lone pairs, whose two lines share no path with a third line. The folded line's word says both ways: one word where the two ways say the same, else both words centred as `a · b`, or, where that does not stand clear, each word beside the end its way arrives at. Its tooltip names both relations, pointing at it lights both, and its origin stays in the tooltip, since its start is a head too. The checker reads a folded line both ways: it traces both relations along it, does not count it as running one way beside or against another line (`two_lane_off`, `opposite_shared`), and traces the two lines' shared route once.

## Legibility

8. **Labels are whole.** A chip is never cut: it is as wide as its label up to 220px, and a longer label wraps onto more lines, so the chip stays inside the edge it sits on and inside the canvas. A name wraps to two lines and widens its box. It is cut only past the hard width (280px for a module box, 220px for a unit box), and then the tooltip carries the full name. No label is wider than its box.
9. **Nothing overlaps.** No two boxes or chips overlap, and no line passes through a box it does not join.
10. **A port sits on the side its lines flow from.** On a unit's frame, a contract the unit provides and a caller from outside sit on the top edge. What the unit uses sits on the bottom edge, even a contract it also provides. While the view is layered, the frame's top and bottom take part as two extra nodes, so a unit that meets a port on top rises toward it and one that uses a port below sinks toward it. A filled chip is a contract the unit provides and an outlined chip is one it uses. A module outside that meets several boxes with no contract would send a line from its chip to each of them, across the page. So the renderer also draws such a view with one dashed chip per box and edge for the modules outside that meet that box, named while the names fit 24 characters and counted as `+N` past that, with every name in its tooltip. Each box then takes one line from its chip. choose keeps that drawing only where it crosses no more than every drawing with neither lanes nor gather and reads better, and the focus views of a gathered view gather their box's chips the same way.
11. **A box's colour names it, and a line wears the colour of the box it leaves only on hover.** Each unit drawn as a box takes one of six colours from a fixed palette (`hues.go`): blue, teal, magenta, purple, cyan and orchid. The box shows it as a 1.5px border in that colour and a fill of the panel tinted 5% toward it. A pointed-at box thickens its border to 2.6px, so it still stands out. A contract drawn as a box, because no module in the view provides it, takes no colour. Two units are joined when some view draws a line between their boxes, whether the line runs through a contract's chip or not. The colours are chosen once, over every view's joins together, so a unit wears one colour in every view that draws it. The unit whose joined units already hold the most colours is coloured first, then the one joined to the most, then by ID, so the same state always gives the same colours. Each takes the colour fewest of its joined units hold, and among those the colour its siblings use least. A unit joined to more than six others could still share one. At rest every line is drawn in the neutral grey, and so are its head, its label and the dots on its ink, whichever box it leaves, a direct dependency that skips the contract boundary included, which keeps the 1.5px of any other line and its open head. A road several boxes' lines ride is that one grey along its whole way, never one rider's colour, so a composite module's lines and a highway read as plain ink and no box's colour stands for another box's lines. The one line in colour at rest is a special line that shows behaviour, the lead, the primary flow's numbered way (see Lead, zones and dress). A box's colour reaches its lines on hover: pointing at a box lights each of its lines whole in the box's colour, and pointing at ink lights every line along it whole in the colour of the box it leaves, or of the box it reaches when it leaves only a frame's chip (`hover.go`). So a reader tells a road's riders apart by hover, by where each rider leaves the road and by the tooltip, which lists every line the ink carries. A word that stands right over another line's flat track would read as that line's, so it is left to the tooltip (`unlabelKin`); a word is placed clear of every flat track first, so few are left to drop. The lint table's `colour_switches`, which counted colour changes along each line at rest, reads 0. A view that draws a lead also thins its other lines, so the lead is the one bold way; a hub's view keeps its lines' weight, since none stands out over them. No palette colour is orange, ochre or green, which a reader with protanopia or deuteranopia would confuse with the warning colour the palette keeps apart. The kind is in the arrowhead and the weight. A use through a contract ends in a filled head. A direct dependency ends in an open head. A unit that provides a contract is drawn 2px thick and ends in a hollow head. A line between two modules that use each other has an open head at each end. Every colour, the grey and the orange stand at least 3:1 against the page and the box fill, in the light theme and the dark one, and every colour stands 3:1 against its own tinted fill. The palette's colours and the grey stand at least 25 apart in CIELAB. Every one of them stands at least 30 from the orange, for normal vision and under a full protanopia or deuteranopia (Machado et al. 2009). Tests hold all three. A contract chip on a box wears that box's colour too: an exit chip tinted 15% toward it, bordered in it and written in it drawn 20% toward the ink, and an entry chip outlined and written in it; a chip's 11px words stand at least 4.5:1 on the tint in both themes, orchid the closest at 4.80 in the dark. A chip on a filled box keeps a border of the panel colour, so it never vanishes into the fill. A pointed-at or focused box draws its border 2.6px in its own colour, the colour its lit lines take, and a filled box darkens its fill instead; a box or chip with no colour takes the accent. A keyboard focus adds a dashed ring, so focus does not rest on colour. The colour never stands alone. The open head says a line skips the boundary, a box's name says what its colour does, and where a line runs says which box it leaves. A line a pstack reader found (판독) starts with a hollow purple dot. A line a human stated (확인) starts with a filled green dot. A line with no dot was extracted from the code (추출). The dot's tooltip says what the word means. 판독 means a reader read the relation, ABBD checks on every sync that the quoted code still exists, and no human has confirmed it.
12. **Unwired contracts are grouped.** A contract that no inside unit is recorded as meeting is not spread along the frame. It is listed below the drawing's text panel as a chip group, with a shared first word shown once (`abbd`: diff, evidence, ...).
24. **A line that passes over another breaks the one beneath.** Where a vertical line crosses a horizontal one, the horizontal line is cut for 4px on each side of the vertical one. That reaches at least 2px past the widest stroke, the 3.2px lead. The cut is a gap with no arc, so it reads as one line passing over the other, and a dot reads as a join. A dot joins only ink of its own kind (rule 17), so the crossing is left whole only where a dot of the kind both lines share sits on it. The horizontal line is always the one cut, whatever order the lines are drawn in, so the same state draws the same gaps. A crossing within 6px of a turn of either line is not cut, since there it reads as one line ending on the other. A crossing within 8px of the end of the horizontal line is not cut either, since the cut would leave a stub under 4px. Nor is a crossing where the cut would also cut the horizontal line's own vertical ink. Two crossings on one stretch less than 12px apart share one cut, so no sliver under 4px is left between them. The cut is an SVG mask on the horizontal line's path (`marks.go`). The mask's id hashes only that line and its holes on the canvas, so a change to one line renames no other line's mask. Its path keeps its points, so the checker's ink graph still reads the crossing as a crossing. Enforced on the rendered SVG. Every such crossing has a hole through the whole width of the stroke beneath, every hole sits on a crossing, and no stretch of ink under 4px is left beside a hole. The fixtures draw 1114 holes at 1268 crossings of two strokes.
25. **A line says what crosses it.** A stored relation may carry a phrase, a reader's few words for what crosses it, such as a route, a message or a query (`HTTP /api/v1`, `schema fetch`, `Cypher`). The phrase is a note beside the edge's claims, with its own writer, and never a claim (`docs/identity-and-precedence.md`). A note may also name up to three `symbols`, the functions, types or channels in the code that carry what the phrase says (`uv_async_send` for a completion notice): a reader's each stand in a quote the note cites, so the code that shows the symbol keeps the note live, and the symbols are kept with the note, said by `abbd explain` and `abbd impact`, and asked for by a `lint: symbols:` line in the text report that never fails and is not in the JSON. The picture does not draw them yet. It may also say how the coupling arises, its `via`, which is a call, data, a convention, a config, an import, a trait, a callback, a macro, a build feature, a link, a load or a spawn. A line prefers the phrase. A line whose relations all carry one phrase says it. Any other line where a relation carries a phrase says, at rest, one name: the first name of the phrase the most relations carry, the first by name among equals, so `Unit, ID, Anchor` reads `Unit`, and only when at least half the line's relations carry that phrase. A phrase fewer carry would name the whole line for a few of its relations, so such a line says, at rest, the via word the most of its relations name, the first by name among equals, when at least half name it, or nothing, and keeps its phrases in the tooltip. Such a via word, like a phrase, is said once along its track, and a line that carries a phrase never takes the passes that let a word stand near the same word. The other lines out of abbd CLI, such as those into internal/topology and internal/narrative, were on the bus's track, which then said 호출, so they kept their word in the tooltip. The fixtures hold no phrase, so their drawings do not change. A line reads one word, as the exemplars' lines do, and never a comma list, 등 or 외 N. A path that stands for several lines, as a bus does, counts lines instead, a line carrying a phrase when any of its relations does. Where that word is already set within 200px on another line, or anywhere on the line's own track, the line says the first name of its next phrase that half its relations carry instead, which tells it from its neighbour, and with no other phrase that stands clear it keeps its phrases in the tooltip only. A track is the paths of one bundle, and lines whose ink touches and that leave a place in common, however far they run, so a phrase is said once along the line a reader follows. The drop that shows an arrival bus's count speaks for every line the count counts, those the bus's other drops draw included. A relation that merged requests share counts once. A line with no phrase whose relations all name one `via` carries that word, in Korean (데이터, 관례, 설정, 트레이트, 콜백, 매크로, 빌드 기능, 링크, 로드, 실행). Call and import are plain vias: a solid line already says 호출 and a dotted one 임포트, so their word stands only in the line's tooltip, as `via 호출` or `via 임포트`, and never at rest, the majority via word included. A line with none of these carries no word. The tooltip of every line lists each phrase its relations carry, each with whose words it is and how many of the line's relations carry it, as in `charge post (판독) 2/10`, so a phrase that names one relation of ten does not pass for the line. A reader's phrase reads 판독 even on a line the script read. The word is set in 11px text of the line's colour (rule 11). A phrase is set in italic and a via word upright, so a reader tells the reader's words from how the coupling arises. It is always set upright and horizontal, never turned, since a turned 10px Hangul word does not read at 1x. It sits above or below a horizontal stretch of the line, 3px off it, or beside a vertical stretch, 4px off it. Horizontal stretches are tried first, nearest where the line leaves first. It keeps 10px from the stretch's ends, where a turn stands. It keeps 2px from every box, chip, the frame, count, dot, origin mark, arrowhead, other label and other line's stroke, and it stays on the canvas. A line parallel to its stretch keeps 8px off it, more than twice the 3px it stands off its own line, so a word never floats between two parallel lines where either could own it. The area it keeps clear covers its glyphs to the half pixel, since a word of odd width is centred on a whole pixel. A bus whose lines all say one word carries it once, anywhere along the bus. Otherwise a line is labelled only on ink that is its own, such as the drop into its box, since a word on a shared trunk would name lines it does not stand for. A word is at most 170px wide, which holds a phrase of 24 lower-case letters. A comma inside brackets, as in `f(a, b)`, does not split a phrase into names. When a first name does not fit, the next phrase by count speaks. A line with no phrase that fits falls back to the via word its relations share, and with none keeps the phrases in the tooltip only, as does a line with no stretch that holds its word. `TestLabelAudit` checks the word each line says, drawn or not, against the lines it speaks for over every view of the fixtures, this repository's own `.abbd` and any root in `ABBD_LABEL_ROOTS`. It fails on a word that lists more than one name or adds 등 or 외 N, on one that is not the first name of a phrase the lines carry, on one that names a phrase or a via fewer than half the lines carry, on a via word shown while a relation carries a phrase, and on a line that holds a relation twice. A phrase is at most 24 characters and 4 words, and it is drawn only while the code its note cites is as quoted and a claim shows on the edge. Enforced on the rendered SVG. Every label is upright, on the canvas, beside a stretch of its own line, and 2px off every mark and line. The fixtures hold no phrase. Of the 507 labels, 243 stand beside a vertical stretch. Summed over its views, 709 relations are drawn, 420 of them on a line with a word, 225 on a labelled line and 75 on a line its own phrase labels. The views carry 154 labels, 7 fewer than without the room from parallel lines, and the overview carries 6 on 65 paths. The lines with a word and no label have no stretch that holds it, which a longer phrase makes more likely. The fixtures hold no phrase, so their drawings do not change.

## Lead, zones and dress

A view reads first by its lead, its bands and its filled box, the way a diagram an engineer polished by hand does (`lead.go`, `zone.go`, `dress.go`). None of them adds a line or moves a box, so every rule above measures the same ink with or without them.

- **Lead.** One way through the view, drawn 3.2px in the colour of the box it starts from, its lines numbered from 1 in a badge. It follows a stored flow where the state holds one: its steps in order from the first the view shows, a step on a box already on the lead adding nothing, each new box joined by the line from the box on the lead that ran a step last and uses it, or by its own line to the step before's box. With no flow stored, it starts at the one box no other box in the view uses and that uses others, and follows the line that stands for strictly the most relations, box by box, until two lines tie. A view where either reading gives fewer than two hops draws no lead. A project draws no lead read from the lines by default, so only the primary flow and stored flows are numbered; `inferred_lead: true` in `.abbd/project.yaml` turns the reading on, and the routing reads the same switch, so a line the lead would ride is not kept in sight for it. `abbd lint` says how many views follow the primary flow, a stored flow or their lines when some view infers its lead, and `--json` gives each view's `lead` and `totals.lead_hops`. A view that lays the primary flow out (O4) follows that flow's stages first, whatever stored flow would run further on it, and numbers only a line with one head that runs from a box on the lead to the next stage's box, so every number reads in stage order: a stage that only a line against stage order reaches, or only a two-headed line, joins the lead unnumbered and the lead goes on from it. Every other line rests thin and grey (rule 11). The badge keeps 3px from every line that is not its own hop's, from every arrowhead, dot and chip, and off every box, label, count and other badge. It sits halfway along its line where it can, else at the nearest fortieth that can, else up to its radius to one side, still touching its line, else 4px further beside it. Numbers are set once every box carries its last line, and a number first takes a place on ink no other hop runs along.
- **Recorded zones.** The stored overview names zones, and the overview draws those that stand as its bands (`recorded` in `zone.go`). A zone stands unless the code under its quotes has moved, as for any stored part. Where at least one stands, the overview draws no band for the layers of a stack or for a folder, since a reader chose the record's zones from those candidates. A STALE zone draws nothing, and a record with no zone that stands draws the bands below, as every view other than the overview does.
  - A zone whose boxes sit together is one band. A zone whose boxes are apart is cut into pieces (`pieces` in `zone.go`): the most boxes one clear band holds, then the most of what is left, and so on, and a box alone is a piece. A band takes in every box of its own zone it holds, so a zone that fits one rectangle is one band however reading order meets its boxes. Among pieces of a size the one whose boxes come first in reading order is taken. Every band of a zone bears its name and the one colour most of the zone's boxes wear, so one name read in several places says that the zone runs across the picture. No box of a zone is left out of its bands.
  - A recorded zone's band takes no number, because a number is a stage's. The primary flow's stage names therefore no longer stand as bands there; the numbers stay on the boxes and the names on the stack's page.
  - A box no zone names stands in no band. A contract no box provides and a library chip keep the bands of their own, whichever zone names them. When two zones name units that one drawn box shows, the zone stored first holds the box.
  - A band that holds a box alone is the one change to the rule that a band holds two boxes. The rule still holds for every other band.
- **Zones.** A tinted band with a small-caps title behind the boxes of one grouping the state shows: a stored stack's layers, numbered from the top (the primary flow's by their stage, as graph.json and the stack's page number them), else the boxes whose code sits in one directory, two at least and not every box, plus the contracts no module in the repository provides, even a single one, titled 외부 계약. On this repository's own overview the manifest stays unbanded, since its band would meet the `6 · vocabulary` band, and a band never meets another. A band is drawn only where its padded area holds no other box, frame chip or band, so it never asks the layout for room. A title is measured as the page sets it, 10px monospace capitals 0.14em apart, so its corners are judged at its true width; it used to be measured about 36% too wide. Its title keeps the corner it has always taken, the corner of its usual room the fewest lines, labels and counts cross, when that corner keeps 3px from every line, arrowhead, dot, chip, label, count and badge. Otherwise it takes the first such place along that room, then the other room, then a room 12px taller on two lines, and with none it stays where it was. On this repository's own overview two still do, `2 · blueprint rendering` and `3 · store`, which no place in their rooms clears.
- **Dress.** The lead's entry is filled with its colour, or, in a view with no lead, a hub: a box that at least four lines and half again as many as any other box touch. A hub's lines rest grey like every other line; the filled box marks the hub, and pointing at it lights its lines in its colour. Every box carries a last line in small capitals, its kind and its role (진입점, 허브, 외부), where its chips leave room. A line of 30px or more whose relations all arise by convention, import, trait, macro or build feature is dotted with round dots, 2px wide, so it never reads as the long dashes of a climbing line; a shorter one stays solid, since a dotted stub under other lines leaves the pointer only its gaps.

- **Card.** A view keeps a small key in a free corner of its own canvas, titled 범례, the way a hand-drawn diagram does: a sample of each mark it draws that few readers would guess, the lead, the filled box, a bypass (its open head), a dotted static line (정적 의존), a climbing line, and nothing it does not draw. It needs two such samples at least, and room 6px clear of every box, chip, line, arrowhead, dot, label, count, number and band, in a corner or along an edge within a third of one, so it never floats mid-edge; a view with no such room keeps only the key below it.

## Flow

13. **Lines read down and right.** The checker takes every line where it arrives at a box or a chip. The line runs forward when its end lies below its start, or level with it and to the right. A line that provides a contract reads from the contract to the unit that meets it, so it counts the other way round. At most one line, or 20% of the arriving lines if that is more, may run back, up or left. A line that runs back is drawn as any other line of its kind, with its kind's stroke and head, and dotted where it arises only in design: which way it runs is the layout's doing, and what it means is its kind. The count says how many lines climb, not how far; the routing weighs the ink of the climbing lines too, so a climb across the whole view costs more than one across a row. On a view that lays the primary flow out (O4), a line from a later stage to an earlier one runs back by the order the stages set, which O4 judges, so the checker leaves it out of the count of back lines, as P1, P2, O1, O2, O3 and H1 leave stage pairs. A path is left out of the count only when every line it carries runs from a later stage to an earlier one. A back line with an end no stage holds still counts, as does every back line on a view that lays no flow out. A road's path may end going up into a box that a line it carries reaches going across, as a two-headed line between boxes of one row does, so where the drawing holds its routes the checker reads such a path by the route that ends there.

    The lint table's `against_axis` counts the lines that run against the axis a view reads by, and `against_axis_ink` their ink in pixels (`againstAxis` in `lint.go`). A line counts when this rule draws it back. A line between two boxes that use each other leaves the upper box, or the left one in a row, so it never runs back. The views together may not raise the lines past their ceiling, and a page the debt file names may raise neither the lines nor their ink. What the count cannot see is an axis that carries no flow: 10 of vector's 11 overview boxes share one cycle of imports, so their rows come from breaking that cycle and say nothing of its pipeline's stages. `graph.json` marks such boxes (`Cycle`, Pages).
15. **Lines take the short way.** No line runs more than 240px longer than the shortest grid path between its ends. A line that passes rows runs down its chain (see below). A line to a frame chip whose chain lies more than 160px off the way between its ends passes the rows on its own instead, since the chips are set out after the layout, and its chain keeps no room. A seated drawing sets the chips in the layout, so each stands over its chain (Chips seated in the layout). A line that passes rows on its own keeps the x it passed the row above at wherever the row lets it, so it turns only where a box stands in its way, steps aside toward its end when it must, and reaches across to its end in the last gap. A line with no chain takes a lane in the space between two boxes, at least 13px from any chain, or passes beside the row straight down where it is headed, a lane outward from any line already there.

The layout keeps rule 13 by layering what uses what: a unit sits above everything it uses, and a cycle's edges are reversed for the layering only (Eades, Lin and Smyth's greedy order), so few lines climb. It is a heuristic, not the fewest. Fewer reversed pairs in the order do not mean fewer dashed lines on the page, because a reversed pair's lines then bundle differently.

The rows are then laid out as a proper layered graph, the way Sugiyama, dot and ELK do (`layered.go`). The data shape is rows of items, where an item is a box or a dummy. Each pair of places more than one row apart gets a chain: one dummy in every row between them, as wide as the lanes its lines need. A frame's top and bottom take part as a row above the first and a row below the last.

- **Ordering.** Each row is sorted by the weighted mean position of its neighbours in the row above, then below, and adjacent items swap while that crosses less. The order with the fewest crossings over all adjacent rows, dummies included, is kept. A box joined to nothing in the rows next to it stands last in its row, so it never pushes aside the lines that pass it. A layer too wide for the budget wraps into rows, and a person stacks those rows by where each box's lines go. The boxes with lines to what lies below, or to the frame's bottom, go in the last row, so their lines leave from the bottom row and pass no box of their own layer on the way down. The boxes with no line below fill the rows above, near the lines that reach them. Each group keeps the order the crossing count chose, and the rows are ordered again with the chains the wrapping adds. This holds only where the boxes with a line below fit in the last row. Where they spill into a row above, some of their lines pass a row of their layer anyway, and the layer wraps in the crossing order alone. Stacking every layer, wrapped or not, and letting the boxes with a line below spill over took the crossings to 330. It also left `shared types and schemas` with a line that turned 14 times, since a box with a line below stood in the middle row and its line wove through the last one to its chip.
- **Ordering three ways.** The crossing count the ordering minimizes leaves out the frame's chips, the buses and the climbing lines, so it is not what a reader sees. A harder search on it, sifting each item to its best place and sweeping twice as long, cut the count by 31 across the fixtures, but only two drawn views changed, and both got worse. So the rows are searched three ways. The first starts from the rows as given and pulls each item to the weighted mean of its neighbours. The second pulls each item to their weighted median. The third starts with every row reversed. Each view of a unit is routed all three ways wide, and the first way packed too (see Room to run straight), and the first way's wide drawing is the best so far. The overview is drawn the first way only (Staircases first). A later drawing replaces the best so far where it reads better by the order in Staircases first. A focus view seats its boxes by its full view, so it is drawn once and never weighed. Six full views take another way. pstack's `setup and help` is one. Its lines ran side by side for 2228px, past rule 14's limit of 2200px, and now run so for 1058px, so it leaves the debt list. The climbing lines stay at 87, since the order in a row does not choose which lines climb. Orca's desktop renderer UI gets worse in one measure. It gains 2 jogs, and loses 5 crossings, 1 bend and 315px of dashed ink. The focus view of `mobile/components` gets worse in five. It seats its boxes where the mobile app's view now stands them. It goes from 0 jogs to 2, from 4 bends to 5, from 304px of braid to 450px and from 3 paths that turn more than twice to 8. It also draws 214px more ink and is 4px wider. The mobile app's view itself loses 16 crossings, 14 jogs and 2109px of dashed ink, and is 16px wider. The desktop renderer UI goes from 13 bends to 12 and still gains 2 traced jogs. Two ways can stand the boxes in the same rows and still place them apart, since the dummies between them stand apart. Only 2 of the 138 extra drawings lay out exactly as an earlier one, so every way is routed. A build routes each full view three times, so building the four fixtures takes about 2.6 times as long, the best of 5 runs going from 0.36s to 0.92s. Orca takes 0.56s where it took 0.20s.
- **Coordinates.** Each box is a block and so is each chain, as in Brandes and Köpf, so a chain is placed as one vertical line and a box steps aside where a chain must pass. Each chain, longest first, is set as few straight runs as it can be: a run grows down as long as the runs keep the row order (two runs that swap sides cannot both be straight) and the drawing keeps its width budget. A chain that cannot be straight all the way turns where it must, not in every row it passes, and one chain that would widen the drawing costs no other chain its straight line. The blocks start halfway between their leftmost and rightmost places. Each then moves, within what its neighbours leave it, toward the weighted median of what it is joined to, with weights 1, 2 and 8 for box to box, box to dummy and dummy to dummy, as in dot. A run that reaches an end of its chain moves toward an x it can drop straight from at that end. A drawing still wider than its budget gives up its shortest chains. Once the rows are placed, an item moves along its row, past up to 8 items, while that saves more than 120px of line measured on the placed drawing, a crossing counted as 120px, and the rows are placed again: a line that would cross the whole drawing to dodge a few crossings reads worse than the crossings, the way a person moves a box to the side its lines go. A move changes the gaps in its row, so when the placing again comes out past the budget and wider than before, the rows stay as they were.
- **Room to run straight.** The blocks used to be packed as tight as their order let them, so the drawing was only as wide as its widest row and a box could move only within that. Now each block may move anywhere within the whole budget toward what it is joined to, and the drawing is then trimmed to what its blocks cover. A box with spare room stands under the box it uses, so their line drops straight, and the rows of a layer spread instead of crowding to the left. Each of the three ways of ordering (below) is drawn wide like this and the first also packed as before, at 920px for a unit's view, and the drawings are weighed as below. The median and reversed orders are no longer drawn packed, nor the median order laned to follow, nor the median order loose: leaving these four out drew 15 evaluation sets, the four fixtures, this repository and five repositories never measured before byte for byte the same. Each full view was then drawn six times, so building the four fixtures took about 1.9 times as long, the best of 3 runs going from 0.98s to 1.83s.
- **Staircases first.** The user decided that a staircase costs a reader more than a crossing, and judges a drawing by the bends a reader sees. So every drawing of a unit's view is weighed by these measures, compared in order, and the first in which two drawings differ decides (`cost.beats` in `weigh.go`). First come the touches, pairs of lines where one ends on the other though not every place on one reaches every place on the other (rule 18). A reader who follows that ink reads a relation that is not there, so they come before everything. Second come the breaks of rules 13 and 15. They are the lines that run more than 240px out of their way, and the back lines past one or a fifth of the arriving lines. Third come the staircase steps, weighed together with the crossings. A staircase step is a turn past a line's second, or a stretch shorter than 24px between two turns (a jog). A line that turns once or twice reads as a corner, and every turn after that is a step a person drawing by hand would not make, however long the stretches between. So a line that turns 8 times has 6 steps, and a line that turns twice with a jog between has 1. The steps are counted along each line's whole way from its start to its end, its buses included. Each pair of places counts once, on its way with the fewest turns, which is how the trace counts a path on the page (`trace.go`). `route` returns the routing with those ways, so the steps are counted on the drawing as routed. One step weighs as much as 4 crossings (`stepCrossings`). So a drawing with fewer steps wins unless it buys each step it saves with more than 4 crossings, and between drawings that weigh the same, the one with fewer steps wins. Then come the paths that turn more than twice, the braid and the ink of the climbing lines, in that order. A tie keeps the earlier drawing, so the choice is the same on every build. The rule breaks come before the steps because without them a jogs-first choice broke four rules. The overview is drawn in the plain order only and is never weighed. Each module's page holds the overview's focus view of it, seated by the overview, so an overview that took another order after a small change redrew every page (A new line can redraw the site).
- **Judged as lint reads it.** The chooser judged the drawings of a unit's view by steps, a measure of its own, while `abbd lint` judges the same drawing by excess turns. The two disagree. A line in a clear column that turns twice has no step but 2 excess turns. So Orca's `src/` kept a drawing that lint weighs at 363, though a seated one weighs 359. Now `judge` in `weigh.go` reads every drawing of a unit's view the way lint does. After the touches and the rule breaks, `cost.beats` compares lint's hard breaches, then lint's weighed sum. In that sum each placement breach and each chip folded into a "+N" chip counts as one more excess turn. The steps and the measures after them break ties. The `mobile/terminal` and `mobile/transport` focus views gain 3 and 5. They seat their boxes by the mobile app's new order, and a focus view is never weighed. `src/` still keeps its plain drawing, since the seated one has 4 more placement breaches. Lint reads each drawing back from its SVG, so a build takes about twice as long. `choose` lints only a drawing that does not already lose on the touches and breaks of its routing, and the check for a line through a box samples each line once and skips the boxes its bounds do not reach. Of the time left in judging, rendering the SVG and reading it back takes about a quarter, and the criteria the rest. Judging from the drawing in memory would need a second reading of the geometry the template draws, and lint must read the drawing as drawn, so the round trip stays. `measure` now skips pairs of strokes whose bounds lie too far apart to cross or run side by side, which leaves every measure as it was and takes the lint of the four fixtures from 4.7s to 3.1s. The candidates of an inside's focus view are judged the same way, as the page will show them: painted in the colours chosen before any candidate is (from the overview, the full views and the first view of each focus box; the page is painted in them too), and dressed, before `judge` reads them. A total that reads the painted colour, such as `combs`, then has the same value in the check and in `abbd lint`.
- **Chips seated in the layout.** A seated tactic sets a unit's wired frame chips as nodes of the layout, as `frame:top|<key>` and `frame:bottom|<key>` (`chipNode`), each as wide as its label. They take no part in the layering, which the frame's top and bottom still steer. They stand in a row above the first and a row below the last, and are ordered and placed with the boxes and the chains. A chain from a chip starts under the chip, and a chip is pulled toward what its lines reach, so a chip's chain and the chip agree in order and in x. Once placed, the chips leave the rows, and each keeps its x as the place `settle` sets it at. A seated tactic is drawn only for a unit's view whose chips fit on the sides rule 10 gives them, and only on the whole budget, with the three ways of ordering. So a unit's view is drawn at most nine times and the overview once. Letting chips steer the order of the boxes took the jogs to 377 and the crossings to 275. Keeping the boxes in the order they take without chips took them to 379 and 286. Seating only the chips with one box below them took them to 375 and 311, and the traced bends up by 100. Seating them on the packed drawings too cut 1 more jog and 3 more crossings and made a build of Orca 25% slower. Building the four fixtures takes 1.71s where main takes 1.83s, best of 3 on one machine, since the overview is now drawn once. Two changes pay for the extra drawings. `weigh` skips pairs of strokes whose bounds lie too far apart to cross or run side by side, and `route` keeps the plain drawing's layout instead of routing the view a third time when the shared buses lose. Both leave every drawing as it was.
- **Steps before excess turns.** Judged on lint's weighed sum first, some views traded crossings for turns a reader does not see as a staircase. A line that turns twice in a clear column has 2 excess turns, worth 8 crossings, and no step. Side by side at 1600px (`eval/lint/shots`) the lint-first drawings of the mobile app and `core principles` read worse than main's: more lines run across the middle band, and `core principles` stacks its long lines in the top right. Now `judge` compares, after the hard breaches, the crossings plus 4 for each staircase step lint traces on the page and 1 for each excess turn, then lint's weighed sum. Both count each placement breach and each folded chip as 4 more. Without the excess turns in that first measure, this repository's `internal/model` took a drawing with 1 crossing fewer and 12 more excess turns at the same steps, and its weighed sum went from 1745 to 1792. With them it keeps its drawing, and the fixtures measure the same either way. The mobile app goes back to 28 crossings and 29 jogs with 215 excess turns, `core principles` to main's drawing byte for byte, `desktop renderer UI` to 17 crossings and 63 steps, and `mobile/terminal` and `mobile/transport` to 16 and 56.
- **One measure for chooser and gate.** The chooser ranked a view's drawings by the crossings plus 1 for each excess turn and 4 for each step (Steps before excess turns), while the gate held the fixtures to a weighed sum of the crossings plus 4 for each excess turn and 9 for each tee. A change that made a drawing the chooser had passed over cheaper flipped the view, and the gate then refused it for the 3 the weighed sum adds to each excess turn. Ranking by the gate's sum instead took the fixtures' weighed sum from 7148 to 7069, but pstack's `restate and catch up` traded 3 excess turns for 7 steps and 2 turn-backs, the detours passed their ceilings in Orca and pstack, the placement debt passed Orca's, and two acceptance cases got worse. So the gate's weighed sum is now what the chooser ranks by, without the placement breaches, which keep a ceiling of their own. With a tee at 1 crossing, a line that drops straight across its own bus saves 2 excess turns and the weighed sum falls by 1, so the tees have a ceiling of their own, self 4, ripgrep 0, Orca 13 and pstack 1. The chooser still breaks ties of the weighed sum by the crossings with 9 for each tee and 4 for each excess turn, and holds a laned drawing to it. Held to the weighed sum instead, this repository's own `internal/model` took a laned drawing with 22 steps where it had 12, since the gathered drawing it keeps was then measured against that laned one.
- **Straight drops between next rows.** The stubs on each edge of a box spread evenly along it, edge by edge. So a line between two boxes in rows next to each other left at one x and arrived at another, and turned twice where the two boxes share a column. Now a line alone in its slot that passes no row between its ends and runs down no chain is pinned on the second edge `route` sets to where its other end already stands, when that x is on the edge and clear. Then it drops straight. That covers a line between boxes in rows next to each other and a line between a frame chip and a box in the first or last row, since the chips stand one gap from those rows. On a box's edge clear means at least `slotGap` from the stubs already set there. A frame's edge keeps no such list, as for a line pinned over its chain, so a pinned chip line there is held only to the edge and to the chains passing the row next to it. No view weighs more than 8 more.
- **Chips.** A chip on a frame's edge, or on a module's edge on the overview, stands over the chain its lines run down, keeping its order and gaps. An edge whose chips wrap or fold stays packed.

## Tracing

A reader answers "what does X use" by following X's lines. Rules 16 to 23 measure that walk. The checker builds the ink graph of a drawing: straight pieces of one kind, which join where one ends on another or where a dot sits, and which only cross otherwise. A reader follows a vertical piece the way it was drawn and a horizontal piece either way, since a bus's track carries lines both ways. A traced relation is the path through that graph, with the fewest bends, from where its line leaves its start to an arrowhead on its end. The relations the checker expects are the pairs of places the view requests before routing, so a relation the routing drops is lost. A relation whose end has no shape on the drawing is lost too. A chip that stands for several frame ports carries every port's id, and ink to it traces each of their relations. The expected pairs are still the renderer's own requests: a stored relation the view never asks for is not caught by this check.

16. **Few bends on a traced path.** Target: at most 4 per relation, and a median of at most 2 per view. One crossing costs a reader about as much as 38 degrees of turning (Ware et al. 2002). Measured, not yet enforced. A line that spans rows runs straight down its chain, so it turns only where it leaves its start's track and where it reaches its end's. A line that steps from one bus to another turns twice more at the step. A jog is a stretch shorter than 24px between two turns, a sideways step a person drawing by hand would not make. The checker counts the jogs on every traced path, and the hand reference has 1 where the renderer has 3 on this repository's overview, down from 14. With `ABBD_JOGS` set it lists each path that jogs, by its turning points.
17. **A dot where a join could pass for a crossing.** Where ink of one kind goes four ways, the renderer draws a dot in the grey of the lines through it (rule 11). There two pieces cross, as where a line crosses onto a trunk that runs on past it, and the dot is the only mark that says they join; a crossing with no dot is cut (rule 24). Where ink goes three ways, a T, its shape already says one line ends on the other, and it takes no dot: the exemplars draw none, and a dot at every split and merge put dozens on a page. A dot counts only for ink of its own kind: it neither joins nor marks lines of another. Every split counts on its own, however close it sits to another or to a box. Enforced: 0 four-way points, and 0 T points a gap cuts close by, without a dot. A page the debt file names may not raise its dots or its ×N (`dots`, `counts`).
18. **No phantom paths.** The ink joins two places only when a relation does, but on a road (rule 4). A road carries lines from different places to different boxes, so at rest it may join two places no relation joins. This is on purpose: each line's route still runs from its own start to its own end, pointing at a box or a road lights each line whole in its colour, and graph.json and the stored relations keep every line's own ends, so neither a reader who points nor an agent who reads the files misreads it. Away from roads, and in a drawing too dense to hold its routes, the rest of this rule holds. An arrival bus is shared only by lines from exactly the same places, or, on a shared bus, by places that each reach every box it feeds. Only one departure track meets an arrival trunk, at its top, and every other line steps between buses where no reader can follow it the wrong way (rule 4). Two-headed lines are drawn apart where that keeps rules 13 and 15 (rule 4). There two modules that use each other are joined by one line that reads both ways. A focus view holds one where a ring box on both sides of X is drawn once, its mutual pair folded; elsewhere a twin keeps its lines one way (Focus views).
19. **Few crossings on a traced path.** Target: at most 3 per relation, and a median of at most 1. Measured, not yet enforced. The rows are ordered on whole paths, chains included. Splitting buses for rule 18 still adds lines, and so crossings, in the densest views.
20. **Long lines get their own lanes.** Every line that passes a row has a dummy there, a slot of its own in the row's order, instead of a corridor beside the row. The beside share measures how much ink runs alongside a row of boxes, with a target under 15%. It counts a straight chain through a row as much as a corridor, so it did not fall with the dummies: 0.088 to 0.111 on this repository's overview, and 0.163 to 0.119 on pstack's.
21. **Boxes keep their size.** The tallest box is at most 1.5 times the median box's height. On the overview a contract chip stands for the contracts one drawn line carries. It is named when it is one contract and counted (`계약 N개`) when it is more, with the names in its tooltip. The contracts no line in the view reaches fold into one `+N 계약` chip. A box may be wide so the lines below it drop straight. Enforced.
22. **A count belongs to one line.** A bus shows `×N` only for three lines or more, as the exemplars draw no counts; hover and the tooltips still show each line. A bus whose every line says one word (rule 25) says the word instead of its `×N`, once that word is drawn on its own ink or on its net's. A word nearby on its track may sit on a sibling branch or the trunk, so the bus keeps its count there. A bus whose lines share only call or import keeps its count, since that word stays in the tooltip (rule 25). A word some of its lines lack keeps the count, so a word never hides another meaning, and a word that finds no room, or is dropped later, gives the count back. A word never stands where such a count stood within 200px of the same word, nor does the same word come to stand near it, so trading counts for words adds no label repeat. Each `×N` sits within 10px of the stretch of ink it counts, at least 5px closer to it than to any other stretch, and at least 5px closer to its own line's ink than to any other line's, so a count drawn across another line does not pass for that line's. A gap that holds a bus's stub or drop keeps 16px between the box and the nearest track, so the count fits beside it. A count never sits on a box or on a frame's chip. Measured: 27 counts across the fixtures' views fail it, 4 of them nearer another line than their own.
23. **Every view has a title and a key.** Target for a later round.

**Walking the roads.** A reader follows a line along a road and loses it where the road turns or parts; a path that runs on straight through a junction is followed best (Ware et al. 2002). So on a drawing that holds its routes, `abbd lint --json` gives each view's `road_turns`, the turns lines take on ink they share with another line, and its `junctions`, the points on each line's way, away from its ends, where another line joins or leaves its ink, counted once for each line that passes them. Two lines that come in from either side and merge onto one trunk meet at a junction too. It also gives the `bent_junctions`, each counted once: the junctions where, of the lines that come in one way, more go out one other way than run on straight though one does, or the same read from where they go out (`roadwalk.go`). Nothing bounds them. Over the 18 evaluation sets they count 5682 road turns, 11720 junctions and 417 bent junctions, about half of them splits and half merges. Most bent junctions are a fork off a stem that stands over the one line dropping straight, or lines that come in from the side onto an arrival trunk, where the box below leaves the most lines no way to run on straight. Three ways to let the road most lines ride run on straight were measured on the roads and dropped. Moving first, of two moves that share as long a stretch, the run fewer routes ride took the bent junctions from 417 to 410 and added 22 steps. Doing that only between two runs that could each move onto the other took them to 413 and added 11 steps. Settling the roads both ways and keeping the second only where it bends fewer junctions with no more steps and no more road weight changed 2 pages and 3 bent junctions, took the roads twice as long, and on one page ran 19px more of two lines on one track both ways.

`eval/render-r1/reference-self.svg` is this repository's overview laid out by hand, the way a person would in draw.io, with the same boxes and relations. `TestReferenceRanksFirst` fails when a measure rates the renderer above it, which would mean the measure counts the wrong thing. That is how rule 21 came to bound height and not area: the hand-laid CLI box is wide so its lines drop straight.

## Drawing criteria

The user set out how a drawing should stand and turn, in six areas: where modules stand, how often a line may turn, how turns and bundles go with relations, where a module that many use stands, where a module that only uses or is only used stands, and where a frame's chip stands. The criteria below make that draft precise. Each one is numbered, measured on the rendered SVG, and named in `abbd lint`'s report (`internal/screen/lint.go`). Where a criterion restates a rule above, it names the rule and adds only what the rule leaves out.

A hard criterion must hold. A repository records the hard breaches its drawings already have in `.abbd/lint-debt.yaml`, by view and criterion, as debt. `abbd lint` exits 1 on a hard breach past its view's debt, and marks each breach in its JSON with `debt` true or false. A target criterion is minimized. Each breach is listed. B1 and B2 add up to the excess turns, the measure to lower, and the placement criteria to the placement debt. The same file sets the ceilings of these totals (What a change is weighed by). Each fixture under `internal/screen/testdata` keeps such a file too, and `TestLintFixtures` holds each fixture to it with the same gate `abbd lint` uses.

The terms are these.

- **Row.** A band of boxes whose heights overlap, counted from 0 at the top. The layout's layer is not in the SVG, so the checker reads rows. A layer that wraps (rule 3) is several rows. A frame's top chips stand above row 0 and its bottom chips below the last row.
- **Column.** Two shapes stand in one column when their x spans overlap.
- **Line.** A traced relation (Tracing), from the shape it leaves to the shape it reaches, along the way with the fewest turns. Its turns are rule 16's bends.
- **Obstacle.** A box or chip, other than the line's two ends and what stands on them, that blocks every way with the line's base turns. Within a column the base way is one straight drop between the two facing edges, anywhere across the span the two ends share. Between columns it is a corner of two turns. It runs down from anywhere along the upper end's edge, across at some height, and down onto anywhere along the lower end's edge. The checker tries every height between the edges on a 4px step and keeps 1px clear of every shape. When no base way fits, the shapes that stand in the band between the facing edges, across the span the base way may use, are the obstacles. When no shape stands there, the reason is that the rows leave no room.
- **Hub.** A box that 3 or more places in its view use, boxes and chips together. Across the 274 views of the fixtures, 604 used boxes have 1 user, 114 have 2 and 189 have 3 or more, and in 228 views the median box has fewer than 3 users. A fixed count keeps a box a hub whatever else its view holds. Of the 303 boxes with 2 users or more, 51 break H1. Of the 189 with 3 or more, 15 do.
- **Source and sink.** A source is a box that uses another box and that no box uses. A sink is a box that boxes use and that uses no box. Lines to the frame's chips do not count, since rule 10 places those.

### Placement

- **P1. A user stands above what it uses.** Target. For each relation between two boxes, the user's row is lower in number than the used box's. A relation whose user stands in the same row or below is a breach, with both boxes. It measures how many rows the user stands short of standing above, 1 in the same row and 2 one row below, allowed 0. Rule 13 bounds how many lines may climb, and O3 says which may. P1 lists every one, but for two boxes joined by one line with a head at each end (a mutual wire, which the overview draws for two modules that depend on each other directly). That line reads both ways, so no row order holds both of their relations, and the pair stands well in one row or in rows next to each other. Counted as two relations, a pair stacked a row apart broke P1 once and a pair in one row twice, so P1 paid a row of height for a direction the line does not have. A pair that stands further apart is one breach, with how many rows it stands past rows next to each other, so P1 still asks it to stand closer. The overview's folding tactic leaves out of its ranking every pair of modules that use each other, also those joined through contracts by two one-headed lines. It need not match P1: such a pair drawn in one row breaks P1 for each line, and the chooser passes over a folded drawing that stands more boxes off what they join than the plain one.
- **P2. Boxes used by exactly the same places stand in one row.** Target. Boxes whose sets of users are equal and not empty form a group. A group that stands in more than one row is a breach, allowed 1 row. This is the placement rule 4 counts on when it gives such boxes one arrival bus that splits in the gap above them. The draft said boxes joined to the same partner. In a dense view nearly every box shares a partner with another, so the criterion takes rule 4's reading. A focus view's twins are left out.
- **F1. A frame chip stands over or under the boxes its lines reach.** Target. The chip's x span meets the span from the left edge of the leftmost of those boxes to the right edge of the rightmost. A breach gives the gap in pixels, allowed 0. The draft said the box its line reaches. A chip whose lines reach several boxes cannot stand over each, so it stands over the span they cover. Rule 10 still chooses the edge. A chip on a module's edge on the overview stands on its box and is not held.

### Bends

- **B1. A line turns no more than its ends allow.** Target. The allowance follows from where the line's two ends stand and from the shapes around them, never from the drawn route, with one exception: a fork, below. A route that leaves on a side, or comes back past its start, buys no turns by it. The checker judges the line from the upper end down.
  - Within one column, when the straight drop between the two ends is clear, the line may turn 0 times.
  - Within one column, when every straight drop is blocked, it may turn 2 times. That is how rule 4's exit bundle runs, out of the box, aside past the obstacle and down. The breach names the obstacle.
  - Between columns, when a corner fits, it may turn 2 times. A line leaves its box's bottom edge and reaches the top edge of what it uses (rules 4 and 10), so between columns it cannot turn fewer than twice. The draft allowed 1, and Staircases first reads those two as a corner and no step.
  - Between columns, when every corner is blocked, it may turn 4 times, and the breach names the obstacle.
  - Between two ends that share a row, it may turn 4 times. No line in the fixtures has such ends.
  - A branch of a fork may turn 2 times wherever its ends stand. A branch is a line between two boxes in rows next to each other. It leaves its box's bottom down a stem that another of the box's lines of its kind drops straight down, turns onto one flat track in the gap between the rows, and drops into its box. Every line down that stem is of that kind, ends in that same row, and turns onto that same track if it turns at all, and no other place's line runs along the branch's stretch of the track. A reader sees one line splitting into a row, as a person draws it, not a line that bends. The exception covers no chip, no trunk that passes a row, no stem that stands over no box, no line that leaves by a stem of its own, and no line that lands on another place's track. That same bent line alone still breaks B1. Measured over the fixtures, the exception excuses 19 lines that break B1 without it, each a branch of a fork with a straight drop down its stem.

  Each line that turns more is a breach, with its turns, its allowance, the turns in excess, and the obstacle or the reason. Measured over the fixtures, 1145 lines break B1. 728 of them have no step, since they turn twice where their ends allow 0. Only 3 lines stand in a column whose drop and corners are all blocked, so allowing them 2 where 4 is the fewest they can turn adds 6 excess turns.
- **B2. No jogs.** Target. A jog is a stretch shorter than 24px between two turns (rule 16). Each line with one is a breach, allowed 0, with its turning points.
- **B3. A line never rides past its turn and comes back.** Target. A line turns back where one stretch runs one way, the next crosses, and the one after runs back the other way, such as a line that runs right along a bus past the drop it needs, steps down a lane and runs back left to it. A hand-drawn line turns where it has to. Each line that turns back is a breach, allowed 0, with how many times and how far it rode past, the shorter of the two stretches that run against each other. Lines that share one turn-back, as the lines of one trunk do, count it once, on the first line traced along it. A back line, one that runs up to its end or leftward along its row, climbs out through a gutter and turns back by its nature, so rule 13 counts it and B3 leaves it out.
- **B4. A line never runs as a doubled track beside itself.** Target. Two stretches of one traced line, or of two subpaths of one drawn bundle, that run the same way in parallel, apart but within 12px, for 24px or more read as one line drawn twice. Two stretches that run against each other are a turn-back, which B3 counts, and a pair of a bundle's subpaths along a pair a traced line already counts is that line's. Each line or bundle with such a pair is a breach, allowed 0, with how many pairs it has.

  The **detours** of a drawing are the turn-backs B3 counts plus the pairs B4 counts. They have a ceiling of their own (What a change is weighed by). Most turn-backs on the fixtures rode around a row on a gutter rather than along one bus, and most of those along a bus came from where a departure trunk that runs on below steps down onto an arrival track: every pair of buses stepped down above its lead line's end, so its other lines rode past their own drops and came back. A pair now steps down on the trunk itself when every place on its arrival bus already reaches every place the trunk leads to below, so the join adds no phantom pair. Otherwise it steps down above its end, or at the clear x nearest it on the trunk's side, and a line whose end lies under the trunk where another pair steps down on it runs straight on down. No line runs beside itself. The turn-backs left ride around rows whose gaps between boxes are full, which is placement, not routing.

The **excess turns** of a drawing are each line's turns past its B1 allowance, summed over the lines, plus every jog. They are not the steps of Staircases first. A step is a turn past a line's second, whatever its ends allow, so a line that turns twice in a clear column has no step and 2 excess turns, and a line between columns that turns 4 times around an obstacle has 2 steps and no excess turn. `abbd lint` still reports the steps, beside the excess turns.

### Bends and relations

- **J1. A bundle splits only next to where it goes.** Hard. A split is a point where ink goes three ways or more, dotted or not, where the lines of its kind arrive as one strand, from one side, and leave it on two sides or more, toward two places or more. It stands in a row, or in the gap above or below a row, where one of those places stands. A frame's chips count as a row. Merges are not held, since rule 4 merges lines from different places midway on purpose. A dot where lines arrive from two sides is a merge, or a merge and a split at once, and is not held either. The draft said a bundle parts only at its ends, which would hold merges too, so the existing rule wins there. Measured, the fixtures have 847 dots whose lines go to two places or more. 49 of them carry lines from two places or more. 789 are splits by the strand reading, and all 789 stand next to a row they split toward, so the tighter reading adds no breach.
- **J2. Every four-way join, and every T a gap cuts within gapJoin, carries a dot.** Hard. This is rule 17. A breach gives the point.
- **J3. The ink joins no two places that no relation joins.** Hard. This is rule 18 held at zero, as the user's draft asks, where rule 18 only measured. A breach gives the two places and the way between them. The phantom pairs stay in the metrics table: 524 across the fixtures on roads. The exemption holds only while the drawing routes its lines. No drawing drops its routes now. A page the debt file bounds also bounds its J3 count as `false_joins`, so a record cannot take a false join on as debt.

### Hubs

- **H1. A hub stands below the middle of its users.** Target. The hub's centre x lies between the leftmost and the rightmost centre x of the places that use it, within 1px. A breach gives how far outside it stands. That it stands below them is P1's.
- **H2. The lines into a hub gather into one trunk.** Target. The forward strokes of one kind that end on a hub belong to one bundle, one drawn net. A breach gives the number of bundles, allowed 1. Rule 4 bundles a kind's lines per edge. H2 holds the hub to one bundle across its edges. The lines that climb to a hub, read from the drawing's routes, are left out, since rule 13 bounds them and O3 says which may climb. Counted with them, 30 of H2's 36 breaches in the fixtures came from cycles. Without them it has 6.
- **Folded hubs.** A line from each box into a shared layer most boxes use says only that they use it, yet it may also carry what the product does: a word a reader wrote, a contract the layer provides, state the box reaches. The overview has no switch to fold such lines. It folds the lines of a common module the classification calls a utility (Common modules, below), and then all of them at once, as its foundation band; it folds no line into a common module that affects the logic, nor into any other hub, so a box never says how many boxes use it while some of their lines still stand. Every folded word stays reachable: the band's tooltip names each box that uses it and the words their relations carry, the common modules' page lists them, and the band's focus view draws every line. Level L still reads its hubs off the plan with every link (Levels: used by at least 3 boxes and half of the others, at most 2), and they are the hubs the common modules are read from.

### One-way boxes

- **O1. A box that only uses stands in the top rows.** Target. Every box in a row above a source, among the boxes that join another box, is a source too. So a source in the second row of a wrapped top layer holds, and a source below a box that is used breaks it. A source whose only frame lines run to bottom chips is left to rule 10, which pulls it down.
- **O2. A box that is only used stands in the bottom rows.** Target. Every box in a row below a sink, among the boxes that join another box, is a sink too. A sink whose only frame lines run to top chips is left to rule 10, which lifts it.
- **O3. A line climbs only to close a cycle.** Hard. A relation whose user stands in a row below what it uses is a breach, unless what it uses reaches the user back through the view's relations between boxes. Rule 13 bounds how many lines climb. O3 says which may.
- **O4. The primary flow's stages stand in order.** Hard. A stack marked `primary: true` names the repository's primary flow, its layers the stages in order. A full view, the overview or a module's inside, that shows two stages or more lays them out by them (`flowOf`): across, as one row that holds only the stages and reads left to right, when the staged boxes side by side fit 1260px, else down, one stage to a row. Every other box stands by its lines against the stages too (`flowRank`): below the boxes that use it, else just above the highest it uses, so a box on a chain from one stage to the next stands between their rows, and the frame holds the top and the bottom. Across, a box a stage uses that uses a stage which does not reach it back would climb whichever side of the spine it took, so it stands in the spine row just before that stage; when the row would not fit with it, the flow reads down instead. The rows are then ordered again with each row of two or more stages held as the stages set it, so the rows under a spine follow its order. On zstd marked primary, CLI, compressor and common runtime, the overview went from 7 hard breaches to none. Each box of a stage stands after each box of the stage before it shown on the view: across, in its row and wholly right of it; down, in a lower row. Each pair of consecutive shown stages that does not is one breach, and the total is `flow_monotone`. By transitivity every two boxes of different stages then stand in stage order, so P1, P2, O1, O2, O3 and H1 leave pairs and hubs the stage record placed to O4, and a line between two stages runs back (rule 13, `against_axis`) exactly when it runs from a later stage to an earlier one. A view that lays no flow out has no O4 breach. A focus view lays out no flow and is not checked by O4: it rings its focus, users above and used below, and keeps each box's left-to-right order from its full view (the seat), so stages that read in order there read in order in the ring's rows. A unit a stage names ranks by that stage in graph.json on every view that draws it, the inside of the box that holds it included.

### The rules

- **R. The rules hold.** Hard. Rules 1 to 15 as `legible.go` checks them, rule 2 as the trace checks it (no relation without ink), and rule 21. Each broken rule is one breach. The fixtures' debt is the views `fixtures_test.go` lists.

### One screen

- **S. The drawing fits one screen.** Target. The screen is a browser viewport of 1600x1000 CSS pixels at 100% zoom. There the drawing box is 1335px wide, so a drawing fits at most 1320px wide. Its height is what the page leaves under the header and the drawing's heading, 118px down, with 22px kept below: 860px, less what the page sets between the heading and the drawing. The overview loses 37px for each kind of question it lists (flows, stacks, tables) and 29px for each question, plus 4px, so three questions of three kinds leave it 658px. A module's page loses 22px to its one-line note and a focus view's page 45px to its two-line note. These were measured on the page at that size. A question that wraps, a smaller window, a larger zoom or a display scaled past 100% (a 1920px screen at 125% is a 1536px viewport) leaves less, so S holds the common desktop case, not every screen. A drawing past that makes the reader scroll to see it whole, and a hand-drawn diagram never asks that. Each view past it is one breach, with its size and how far past it runs. The **overflow** is the number of such views. It has a ceiling of its own, held like the weighed sum. The overview is drawn with its plain ranking unless a folded or entering one (`overviewTactics`) fits where the plain one does not, or has fewer hard breaches, and stands no more boxes off what they join. Whether a drawing fits is weighed right after its hard breaches, and how far past it runs only after what lint weighs.

### Levels

- **L. Each full view stands at one level.** Report only. The overview and every module's page have a level, and a feature's page does not. The overview draws 4 to 12 modules, the parts an engineer names when explaining the system, and a contract no drawn module provides does not count. A nested module's page draws at least 2 boxes, a top-level module's page 0 or at least 2, and any page at most 16 (rule 1). A view of 4 or more boxes draws at most 2 lines a box, past the lines into a hub. Only lines between two of the view's boxes count, once whichever way they run, never those to a frame's chips or to a loose contract. A hub is a box at least 3 boxes and half of the others use, such as shared types, listed as `hubs`, at most the 2 most used, and none when as many boxes qualify as do not. The first `abbd lint --record` of a repository refuses while a view with hard breaches has a level line, unless `--keep-level VIEW=REASON` keeps it, which writes the reason under `levels` in the debt file. `abbd lint` prints a `level` line for each view that misses, with what to change in the units, and `--json` lists every full view under `levels`. It never fails lint and is never debt, since the renderer cannot fix it: the reader's choice of units does (abbd-setup, step 1).
- **Words at rest, as a diagnostic.** Report only. The canonical method gives every line a word (C4). On the overview, while the record is current and `overview_cut` is not turned off, a line's word at rest is the record's word for the pair of boxes it joins, the first line the record gives that pair whichever way it runs, so both directions of a pair share one word and count as one pair. Elsewhere, and on the overview for a pair the record gives no word, it comes from a phrase on one of its relations, else from its via when that is no plain call (rule 25), so a repository with no phrases and no record leaves most lines unsaid. `abbd lint` prints `lint: words:` for the overview: how many of its routes say a word at rest, how many of those words are the record's, phrases and vias, and of the pairs the record gives a word, how many label a drawn route; then the same over every view. `--json` gives `views[].words` and `totals.words`. A route takes the word of the first wire that labels it by record, then phrase, then via, and is labelled as `labelled` counts it.
- **Cycles, as a finding.** Report only, with no ceiling and no debt. Boxes that use each other round a cycle have no order between them, so a view cannot read them down by their uses (Rank's `Cycle`). That is the code's doing, not the drawing's, so it is reported as a finding and never drawn in a style of its own. Each full view's cycles are the strongly connected components of two boxes or more of the graph its layout ranks; the overview's are read with every line drawn, whatever `overview_cut` or the record leaves undrawn. `abbd lint` prints a `cycle VIEW:` line for each, its boxes by name, and `--json` lists them under `levels[].cycles`. `abbd status` prints `cycles: N on the overview` and a `cycle overview:` line for each, planning the overview alone without routing any view. Neither fails lint or `--check`, since only a change to the code ends a cycle.
- **Tangles, as a finding.** Report only, with no ceiling and no debt, and the drawing is the same with or without it. Four boxes or more of one full view that each use every other, and are each used by every other, are an exact mutual clique (`mutualCliques` in `matrix_order.go`, the same cliques the matrix looks for, whether or not the view gets a matrix; largest first, never sharing a box, counted over the lines the view draws as the matrix counts them). Such boxes cannot be told apart by what they use, so the code is tangled there, and no layout makes it plain. `abbd lint` prints a `tangle VIEW:` line for each, its boxes by name, and `--json` lists them under `levels[].tangles`. Neither fails lint or `--check`, since only a change to the code ends a tangle. Over the 22 evaluation sets it stands on 3 views, all in orca (MOD-0007 and MOD-0042 with four boxes, MOD-0072 with five).
- **Shape, as a finding.** Report only, with no ceiling and no debt, and no drawing or page changes. `abbd lint` prints a `shape VIEW:` line for each full view that has parts, and `--json` gives the same numbers under `levels[].shape`. They are read off the lines the view stands for, those it draws and the pairs it counts without drawing, by structure alone: no box is told by its name. The parts are the view's boxes; `joined` are those a line touches and `pairs` the pairs a line joins. `cyclic` are the joined parts that stand on a cycle of the lines. `chain` is the most lines one use runs through from a part nothing above uses, a cycle group counting as one part. The layer candidates (`layers`) put each part in the layer of the longest chain of uses above it, so every line between two layers runs down (`down`), and `same` counts the lines inside a cycle group, which have no order. `down` and `same` count each direction of a pair, `pairs` once. A stored stack that puts two parts of the view or more into two layers or more is named with the lines of the view that run up its layers (`stacks[].up`). A family candidate (`families`) is four parts or more that no line joins and that use or provide the same contract, one group for each contract. Where a view is an inside, `contract` and `direct` count the lines that cross its frame through a contract chip and to another module, and `no_provider` counts the contracts a view draws loose with no module providing them. `notes` say in words what the numbers suggest: a view where half the joined parts or more stand on a cycle has no layers to read; a chain of three lines or more with a fifth of the joined parts on a cycle at most is a layer candidate when no stack is stored for it; a family candidate and a contract no module provides are named. Nothing here decides what a view is. The numbers are a finding for the reader who records the view, since a depth exists in any graph and says nothing of whether the code has layers; the reader quotes the code that names them. Over the 22 evaluation sets, `levels[]` is the same as before apart from the new field, and 241 of 289 views carry a shape.
- **Elements, against each view's own range.** Report only, with no ceiling and no debt (`elements.go`). Störrle sizes a diagram by its shapes, line segments and labels, and finds that understanding falls as the count grows. `abbd lint` counts each view's elements as a reader meets them at rest: the boxes and chips (a folded row's `+N` chip once), the runs of ink, the words on lines and beside the lead's hops, and the badges (`×N`, the lead's hop numbers and stay pill). A run is straight ink as a reader sees it: stretches on one row or column that overlap or meet end to end read as one, whichever paths draw them. So a trunk that several lines share counts once, and every bend starts another run: a view with fewer bends or more merged ink counts less. The bends within drawn paths are reported beside the runs. Left out of the count are what does not name a part or a relation: the legend card, zone titles, a box's kind line, arrowheads, junction dots and origin dots. One number for every view misjudges both ends, so the count is read against the view's own range. Its **min** is the skeleton: its shapes, each line the primary flow rides as one straight run, and the flow's numbers. Its **max** is every line it could draw between its places, one per pair and kind it joins, the ones `overview_cut` lists under the picture included, drawn apart: each traced line with one run more than the turns rule B1 allows it, each counted line with three, and each with a word, plus the lead's numbers and step words. Stored relations lifted onto one pair count once, since no view draws them apart. Drawn ink that bends more than B1 allows, and the `×N` badges, which the max leaves out, can take a view past 100%. Since B1's allowance reads where the boxes stand, a change of placement can move the max as well as the count. The view's position is `(count - min) / (max - min)`, rounded up, and it is reported past `WarnShare`, 85% of its range, when `100*(count - min) > 85*(max - min)`. The share is set from the positions of the 829 views with a range among 875 views of 15 repositories: their deciles, in the percents lint reports, run 43, 50, 50, 50, 53, 59, 66, 72 and 84%, and 85% lists 81 views, about one in ten, where 75% would list 108 and 90% would list 42. The 6477 lines of those views draw as 7906 runs with 2135 bends. Vector's `vector-lib` draws its 98 lines as 46 runs and stands at 100 of 28..408 (19%), and the 15 overviews stand between 16% and 73%. The text gives each view's count and range on an `elements:` line in its block, sums them, and lists the views past their point; `--json` gives `views[].elements` and `totals.elements`. A view at its full range can still be small: a focus view of 4 shapes and 3 straight worded lines stands at 100%, and 80 of the 81 views listed hold 60 elements or fewer. Rule 1 and level L keep bounding the size itself.
- **Symmetry, read as a diagnostic.** Report only (`symmetry.go`). A reader recalls a drawing better where what belongs together lines up (Marriott et al. 2012; Purchase et al. 2010). `abbd lint --json` gives, as `views[].totals` and `totals`, each view's `rows_off`, the rows of two boxes or more whose centres do not stand on one line, its `parents`, the boxes that use two boxes or more of the row right below, and its `off_centre`, those of them whose centre stands off the middle of those boxes by more than a quarter of the span their centres cover, so the bound scales with each parent's own span. The text report leaves them out. Over the 801 views of 18 evaluation sets they count 28 rows off, 270 parents and 51 off centre: 25 of them on units' insides, 17 on focus views and 9 on overviews. Symmetry was tried as a tie-break and dropped. As the last measure `cost.beats` compares, it changed no page, since no two drawings of a view tie on every other measure. As a redraw that stands each such box over the middle of the boxes below as far as its neighbours let it, kept only where it stands more symmetric, no wider, crossing no more and reading no worse on `beats` or any total the gate holds, it changed no page either: of 253 redraws, 11 stood more symmetric, and every one of them read worse on the gate's totals, 8 also on `beats`.
- **Twins, as a diagnostic.** Report only (`lint.go`). `abbd lint --json` gives, as `views[].totals.twins` and `totals.twins`, the boxes a focus view draws a second time (the boxes whose ID ends in `↓`): a unit that both uses the focus and is used by it stands above among the focus's callers and its twin below among what the focus uses. No other total counts them, so a change that drops a twin cannot be told by the gate's sums. Nothing bounds it.

### What a change is weighed by

`abbd lint` gives each view, and all of them, these totals. They are the hard and target breaches, the breaches of each criterion, the excess turns, the placement debt, the crossings, the weighed sum, and the path measures of the trace. The **placement debt** is the breaches of P1, P2, H1, H2, O1, O2 and F1 added up. The **weighed sum** is the crossings plus 1 for each excess turn and 4 for each staircase step (`stepCrossings`), the measure the chooser ranks a view's drawings by (One measure for chooser and gate), so a step saved may cost at most 4 crossings. A **tee** is a point where a line's straight drop crosses a flat stretch of another line from the same place, inside both, with no junction dot: a T a reader takes for a branch, a relation that is not there. Each tee counts as one of the crossings, and `route` keeps such a line on its bus (`eval/lint/decisions.tsv` row 92). The **tees** have a ceiling of their own, since the weighed sum prices a tee at one crossing, less than the two turns it saves. Every change that made a drawing the chooser had passed over cheaper could then flip a view to one the gate weighed more. The **excess turns** have a ceiling of their own too. The weighed sum prices an excess turn at one crossing, so a change that bends two lines twice more to save three crossings weighs one less, while the reader follows the bends a hand drawing leaves out. A change to the renderer is held by this gate, and `abbd lint` holds a repository to it.

This list is the gate. The skills, the README and `eval/lint/compare.py` point here and do not restate it. `internal/screen/gate.go` holds it as code.

1. **Hard debt.** No view has more hard breaches of a criterion than its debt records.
2. **Ceilings.** Over every view, each of these totals stays at or under its ceiling in `.abbd/lint-debt.yaml`:
   - `weighed`, the weighed sum.
   - `tees`. A T reads as a branch to a place the line does not reach.
   - `excess_turns`. A bend the drawing does not need is the first thing a reader of a hand drawing notices.
   - `detours`, the turn-backs B3 counts plus the pairs B4 counts.
   - `against_axis`, the lines that run back by rule 13, pages the debt file does not name included.
   - `two_lane_off`, the ink of lines that run opposite ways side by side other than as a two-lane road.
   - `opposite_shared`, the ink two lines run on one track opposite ways.
   - `overflow`, the views that break S.

   None of them may rise, whatever a change saves elsewhere.
3. **Placement.** The placement debt stays at or under its ceiling plus 2% of it, rounded down. A change that saves turns may move a few boxes, and the next kept change starts from the new total.
4. **Page bounds.** A page the debt file names under `pages` may not raise its own `colour_switches`, `parallel_ink`, `two_lane_off`, `abreast`, `combs`, `doglegs`, `counts`, `dots`, `label_repeats`, `density`, `false_joins` (J3), `against_axis` or `against_axis_ink`, and may not lower its `labelled`. Over every view, a page the debt file names that no view draws fails too.
5. **Tie-breakers.** The traced bends and the paths that turn more than twice break ties. Between two changes that weigh the same, the one with fewer wins. Neither has a ceiling. The phantom pairs have no ceiling either; J3 holds them at their debt.
6. **A look by eye.** Any view whose weighed sum rises by more than 8 needs a look by eye, even when the totals fall.
7. **Record.** After each kept change, `abbd lint --record` lowers the debts and the ceilings to what the drawings measure. It never raises a ceiling. A total the gate lets pass its ceiling, as the placement debt may by 2%, keeps the ceiling it had, so the slack cannot ratchet a ceiling up one kept change at a time. Raising a ceiling is an edit to the file that a review sees.

`abbd lint` checks 1 to 4 over every view and fails when one breaks. With a VIEW it checks 1 and 4 for those views only. `TestLintFixtures` checks each fixture against its own `.abbd/lint-debt.yaml`, and fails as well when a debt or a ceiling stands above what the fixture measures, so a kept change always lowers them. `eval/lint/compare.py` checks 2 and 3 between two fixture tables for every total the tables carry, and lists the views for 1 and 6.

The gate's numbers are diagnostics, not an independent score. The chooser that picks each view's drawing reads the same totals, so a drawing that does well by them was in part chosen for it. Whether a drawing reads well is judged by blind reader tests, and the numbers only say where to look.

`ABBD_LINT_FIXTURES=<file> go test ./internal/screen -run TestLintFixtures` writes the per-view totals of the fixtures as a table, and `ABBD_LINT_BREACHES=<file>` every breach. `ink_crossings` (`busy.go`) counts the points where ink passes through ink however it is cut into paths, since a road cuts its ink at other points than a bus did and the crossings above leave out any within 26px of a path's end. `eval/lint/compare.py` totals two such tables, checks the second against the gate's totals, and lists the views that get worse and those that need a look. `abbd lint --list` names the views, and `abbd lint --json` prints the report with a `schema` version, each breach's boxes and chips by unit ID and name, and the gate's failures. The overview breaks rule 15: abbd CLI's line out round the right side runs 898px longer than the shortest way between its ends. `internal/topology` broke rule 13, as Known gaps says, until its lines rode roads.

The gate weighs totals, so a change can pass it and leave the very line the user circled as it was. `eval/lint/acceptance.tsv` turns each repeated complaint, from a screenshot or a review, into one exact measure on one fixture view. Each case measures the traced lines themselves, never the lint's breaches, so a change to a criterion cannot move a case. `python eval/lint/accept.py OUT` runs the fixture lint, `TestAcceptWays` (every traced line with its turning points and its ends' boxes) and the fixture pages in one go test, hovers the pages with `shoot.py` unless `--no-hover`, and prints each case's value against its target and how many of the measured cases fail. `--record` rewrites the table's recorded column with the commit it was measured on. A hillclimb names the cases it targets and reports them beside the gate.

## Figures

A flow, a stack and a table each answer one question with a figure: a question, a caption and parts. A flow's parts are steps, a stack's are layers and a table's are rows. All three keep these rules, and the sections below add what each one does on its own.

- **Who wrote each part.** A figure is a file under `.abbd/flows/`, `.abbd/stacks/` or `.abbd/tables/`, written by `abbd flow KEY FILE`, `abbd stack KEY FILE` or `abbd table KEY FILE`. Each part records who wrote it. A reader's part is INFERRED and a human's, with `--source`, is USER_STATED, and the figure is USER_STATED only when a human wrote every part; in a mixed figure the page marks the human's parts. A reader may replace only a human's STALE parts: each current human part must come back as written and in its order, and keeps the human's word.
- **Retired parts.** A part that went STALE is kept in the file's `retired` list with the code it was written against. A reader's part that says the same words, in any case or spacing and on any unit, is refused while it cites a file whose code changed under the retired part, however many stores ago that part left the figure. A human's store, with `--source`, is not refused and clears the retired parts it says again. abbd forgets a retired part once its code is back as it was, its unit leaves the state, or every file it cites is gone. This shows only that the same words do not come back unread on changed code. It cannot show that a reader who rewords a part read the code again. Deleting the figure's file, or a hand edit that deletes `retired`, forgets the retired parts.
- **Evidence.** Every part cites the code it rests on, each quote held once in its file. One quote at least lies in code that the part's own unit, or a unit inside it, anchors more closely than any other unit does, or that a contract the part's unit provides anchors so, as a command's handler is the code of the command line that provides it. A quote in a file several units share counts for the unit whose anchor names it best. An anchor with a symbol holds only a quote that names that symbol. The quote's `symbol` is the reader's claim: abbd ranks the quote by it and does not check that the quote lies in that symbol's code. A root anchor of `.` holds only the files no closer anchor names. Each part is stamped with the lines around its quotes, so an edit there stales that part alone.
- **STALE.** A part whose unit the state lost, whose code moved, or whose quotes all lie outside its unit's code is STALE, and `abbd status --check` fails until the figure is read and stored again.
- **No units of its own.** A figure stores no unit and no relation. Its parts name stored units.
- **Drawn in HTML.** A figure is drawn in HTML, not SVG, so the browser wraps every line and no label is cut.
- **Linked into the tree.** A unit in a figure opens its own inside, or else the inside of the nearest unit that holds it. Each figure has a page of its own, which marks its STALE parts. The overview lists every figure by its question, with how many of its parts are STALE.

## Flows

A layered view answers "what uses what". A flow answers one question about the order things happen in, such as what `abbd sync` does. It is the numbered figure of the per-module docs agents write: one question, a caption, and steps in execution order, each on the unit whose code runs it, with what the step does, a note (a cache, a TTL, a skip rule), how it fails, and the units it calls.

- **Stored as facts with evidence.** A flow is `.abbd/flows/<key>.md`, and it keeps the figure rules above. A retired step keeps no `calls`. A unit the step calls lends it no code. A call is drawn only where a live stored relation joins the step's unit, or a unit inside it, to the called unit, so a chip never draws an edge the graph lacks. A step whose call the state lost, or that cites no code, is STALE too.
- **Drawn in HTML.** The figure is a numbered list. The steps read top to bottom in their order, and the only lines are the arrows from each step to the next: no crossings, no bends. Consecutive steps on one unit share one cell beside them, which groups by the stored unit only. At 560px and below the cell stands above its steps.
- **Linked into the tree.** A call opens as a step's unit does, and a contract with neither opens the inside of the module that shows its notes. The overview gives each flow's step count, and each module's inside lists the flows with steps on the units it holds, by step number.

## Stacks

A flow answers in what order things happen. A stack answers what the layers of a part of the code are, such as the layers of the abbd CLI. It is the layer figure of the per-module docs agents write. It has one question, a caption, and layers from the top down, each a named layer with a one-line role and the stored units it holds.

- **Stored as facts with evidence.** A stack is `.abbd/stacks/<key>.md`, written by `abbd stack KEY FILE`. It keeps the figure rules above, with a layer's name and role in place of a step's words. A retired layer is forgotten once one of its own units leaves the state. A store warns on a role over 80 characters, as on a step's `does`. A layer holds each unit it names and every unit inside it, and a unit sits in one layer only. So abbd refuses a draft that names a unit in two layers, or names a unit in one layer and a unit that holds it in another, or names both in one layer. A stack has two to eight layers, and its layers name 16 units at most. A layer's quote may lie in the code of any of its units. A stack invents no grouping, because a layer is a list of stored unit IDs. A stored stack that holds a unit twice after the unit moved under another is STALE too. In a human's stack, the layers such a break names are open to a reader as a STALE layer is. The reader may replace them, and any of them that comes back as written keeps the human's word, so CI does not wait on the human.
- **A stack may compose one view.** A stack that says `view` (`overview`, or a module ID), `kind` (`layers`, `stages`, `family` or `core`), a layer's `as` and `promises`, and `faces` is a composition record of that view; a stack that says none is the plain stack above, with the same meaning and bytes. The kind says what the order of the layers means: `layers` a layer uses the ones below it, `stages` the layers run in order (what `primary: true` already said, so a primary stack is `stages`), `family` each layer holds siblings that keep one contract, `core` each layer is what the relations make of the view. A layer's `as` is `layer`, `stage` or `members` by its kind, and for `core` one of `boundary`, `cycle`, `clique` or `hub`. `promises` are the stored contracts a layer keeps for the others, and `faces` the contracts the view offers outside. A box no layer names is allowed; abbd only reports how many are left as lines. A view has one record. abbd recomputes what the relations can bear out, both when the stack is stored and each time the state moves: a `cycle` layer's boxes close a cycle, a `clique` layer's boxes all use one another both ways, a `hub` is one box used both ways with half the view's boxes, a `boundary` box has a relation with a unit outside the module, a promise is provided by its layer and used by another, a family's siblings are each tied to a promised contract, and a face is provided from inside the view. Only a use (`consumes`, `depends_on`) that says its `via` counts, wherever a claim rests on one, since one that does not may be a static dependency and would make a tangle of it; a `provides` counts as stored. A cycle, clique or hub is read from uses that run directly from one box to another; a use of a contract does not link its user to the contract's provider, and a `provides` of a contract homed outside a module is no boundary, so a claim that runs through a contract is not borne out and the boxes are left ungrouped. A store refuses a claim the relations do not bear out, and a stored one they stop bearing out reads STALE. A group is addressed `stack-KEY.N`, as its page anchors it. The overview's own record is not drawn yet. A module's view with a record of kind `core` whose claims all hold (the stack is known, has no gap and no STALE layer) draws each `cycle`, `clique` or `boundary` layer of boxes the view draws directly as one folded box (`compose.go`): the layer's name, and on its last line what its inside holds, `↻ 안에 순환 N상자` for the boxes that use each other round a cycle, else `바깥과 이어진 N상자` for boxes that reach outside the module, else `N상자`, whichever part fits. The folded box is a module for the drawing only: the lines of its boxes lift onto it, and it stands in the order of its first box, since the layout breaks ties by ID and a group is no later than the box it replaces (its drawing ID is that box's ID, a tilde and the group's address). It has a view of its own at the group's address `stack-KEY.N`: a page `stack-KEY.N.html` that draws its boxes as any module's inside is drawn, goes up to the module it stands in, is listed under that module in the navigation, and is linted as a view. The boxes' own pages stay. `abbd explain stack-KEY.N` says what the group is and holds and whether it is drawn as a box (and, if not, why: an overview record, a kind other than `core`, a STALE record or group, a box another record folds first), `abbd explain ID` says which group holds a unit, and `abbd impact stack-KEY.N` reads the group's boxes as the seeds. A view with no such record is drawn byte for byte as before. On the orca remodelling copy (via filled, two groups of the desktop main process) the view's crossings go from 30 to 4, its parallel ink from 724 to 99, its weighed sum from 204 to 136 and its lines from 70 to 39, and its one hard breach (rule 13) stays.
- **Breaches.** A breach is a live stored relation by which a unit a lower layer holds uses a unit a higher layer holds. Only `consumes` and `depends_on` are uses. A `provides` is no breach. It runs from a lower unit to a contract an upper layer homes, so the lower layer serves the upper one, which is dependency inversion and not a use. A relation within one layer, one that skips layers downward, and one with an end no layer holds are no breach. abbd derives the breaches from the state each time and never stores them. `abbd stack KEY FILE` says how many it found, `abbd status` lists each with its ends, their layers and the relation's origin, and the page draws each one. A breach never fails the check. It is debt in the code or a layer drawn wrong, and only a design call clears it. The stack marked the primary flow has no breaches: its layers are stages read upstream to downstream, and a later stage that uses an earlier one, as every stage uses the config, is how a flow runs. abbd neither counts nor draws them there, and its page says it is the primary flow.
- **Drawn in HTML.** The figure is a list of bands from the top down. Each band shows its number, name and role beside the units it holds. A band's breaches sit under its units as chips marked ↑, each naming its two ends and the higher layer it reaches. At 560px and below the band's name and role stand above its units.
- **Linked into the tree.** A stack's page also marks its breaches. The overview gives each stack's layer count and how many breaches it has. Each module's inside lists the stacks whose layers hold its units, by layer number.

## Tables

A flow answers in what order and a stack answers in what layers. A table answers which part does what to which thing, such as which part of the CLI reads and writes which `.abbd/` file. It is the matrix figure of the per-module docs agents write. It has one question, a caption, a head of columns, and rows. Each row is one stored unit with a short statement in each cell it fills.

- **Stored as facts with evidence.** A table is `.abbd/tables/<key>.md`, written by `abbd table KEY FILE`. It keeps the figure rules above, with a row's cells in place of a step's words. A human's column head is the human's word, as the question and the caption are. A reader may change only a column whose unit left the state, by dropping its unit or pointing it at another. A retired row's cells may not come back on its changed code under any column, so renaming a column does not make them new. A table has two to 8 columns and two to 16 rows. A column is a short label of 32 characters at most, named once, and it may name the stored unit it stands for. A row names its unit and fills one cell at least. A column the row leaves out is an empty cell. A unit heads one row, and every cell sits under a column the head names. A store warns on a cell over 80 characters. A stored table whose column names a unit the state lost is STALE too.
- **Cells read against the relations.** A cell under a unit's column says what the row's unit does to that unit, so abbd reads it against the strongest live stored relation from the row's unit, or a unit inside it, to the column's unit or a unit inside it. A cell that says something with no relation under it is unbacked. An empty cell over a relation is unsaid. abbd derives both from the state each time and never stores them. `abbd table KEY FILE` says how many it found, `abbd status` lists each with its row, its ends and the relation's origin, and the page marks each one. Neither fails the check. The graph may lack a coupling a reader can record, or the cell may be read wrong, or the table may miss a use, and only a reader of the code can say which. A cell under a plain column is read against nothing.
- **Drawn in HTML.** The figure is an HTML table. The head names each column, and a column that stands for a unit links to the inside that shows it. Each row's head is its unit, a chip that opens its own inside, or else the inside of the nearest unit that holds it, with the row's quotes folded under it. Every cell wraps. A cell under a unit's column carries the relation's verb as its title. An unbacked cell has a dashed outline, and an unsaid cell shows ◦ with the relation's verb. At 560px and below each row folds to one card, its unit on top and each filled cell under its column's label.
- **Linked into the tree.** A table's page also marks its unbacked and unsaid cells. The overview gives each table's rows and columns and how many cells are unbacked or unsaid. Each module's inside lists the tables whose rows its units head, by row number.

## Overview record

Flows, stacks and tables each answer one question. The overview record answers the question a person asks first: what does this system do, in what order, and in which parts. It is the summary a person sketches by hand, with one primary flow in numbered stages, a few named zones and as many chosen lines as understanding needs, up to 20, each line with a word. It lives in two places, each stored once. The primary flow is the stack marked `primary: true` (O4), its layers the stages in order. The zones and the chosen lines are `.abbd/overview.md`. A reader chooses all of them from the code and the stored relations. abbd checks them and stores them. The renderer lays the overview out by the primary flow, and draws the chosen lines between the boxes they join, each with the record's word, while the record is current; a project that turned `overview_cut` off draws every line and the record gives none its word. graph.json gives the zones and the lines either way, and the overview draws the zones that stand as bands (Recorded zones, in Lead, zones and dress).

- **The primary flow is a stack.** `abbd stack KEY FILE` stores it with `primary: true`, one stack at most, and everything a stack's layer obeys holds for a stage: a name and a role, the units it holds and the code that shows they belong together, each unit in one stage, each part stamped and STALE when its code or its units change. A stage may name a unit inside a box, so that the stage orders the inside it crosses. The box on the overview then stands at the first stage that names it or a unit inside it.
- **Zones and lines are stored as facts with evidence.** The overview's own record is one file per project, `.abbd/overview.md`, written by `abbd overview FILE`. `abbd overview` with no FILE prints a draft to write it from (Writing the record, below). Its frontmatter holds `zones` and `lines`, and its body is an optional caption in the project's language. A draft that names any field abbd does not know is refused, and one that names `flow` is told that the primary flow is the stack marked primary. A zone is a `name`, the stored `units` it holds and the `evidence` that shows they belong together, as a stack's layer is without a role. A line is `from`, `to`, a `word` and `evidence`. There are at most 8 zones and at most 20 lines, and both are optional. A zone names only units the overview draws as boxes: a unit whose parent is not present, a group sync made included. A unit inside another is refused, and the refusal names the box to use. A zone holds each unit it names and every unit inside it. A unit sits in one zone at most, by the rule a stack's layers follow. Zone names, like a line's word, follow the phrase rule: one line, at most 24 characters and 4 words. Two zones may not share a name. A line joins units the overview draws in two boxes, so neither end holds the other and the line never runs inside one box. A line draws only stored relations. At least one live relation runs from `from`, or a unit inside it, to `to`, or a unit inside it, in that direction, or to a contract at the top level that `to`, or a unit inside it, provides. The overview draws such a contract as a chip on its provider's box and the use of it as a line from the user's box to the provider's, so the record reads it the same way. graph.json gives the kinds and vias of the relations under each line, and the line stores neither. A pair of ends is drawn once in each direction. Every part cites code, each quote held once in its file. For a zone, one quote at least lies in the code of its units, by the rule a layer follows. For a line, one quote at least lies in the code of an end, or in a file the evidence of a relation under the line cites.
- **STALE, not refused, once stored.** Each zone and line is stamped with the lines around its quotes, as a figure's part is, so an edit there stales that part alone. A part whose unit left the state, or whose code moved, is STALE. So is a zone whose unit moved inside another, a line whose ends came to nest, a line whose relations all vanished, and a stored overview that holds a unit in two zones after the unit moved under another. `abbd status` prints `overview:` with its counts and a `stale:` line for each STALE part, and `--check` fails until a reader stores the overview again. `abbd evidence` lists each quote of the overview as live, drifted or dead. There is no confirmation step. What vouches for the overview is the evidence each store checks and the staleness each sync reports. An `.abbd/overview.md` abbd cannot read, such as one with broken frontmatter, is no overview: `abbd status` warns and `--check` still passes, as for a stray note, and graph.json then gives no zones or lines until the file is fixed or stored again.
- **Ambient marks.** A reader may mark up to 8 boxes the overview draws as ambient, in the frontmatter's `ambient` list: a `unit`, how it is ambient in `as`, `global` (one instance every part reaches), `static` (fixed data every part reads, such as configuration or shared types) or `context` (a value handed down every call), and `evidence`. A mark is the product's own, unlike a scope mark, and stores no unit and no relation. One quote at least lies in the marked box's own code or a unit inside it. It is refused when it names a unit inside a box, a way there is none of, a unit marked twice, or a unit a scope mark says is no part of the product, and it is stamped and goes STALE as a zone does. `abbd status` counts the marks beside the zones and lines, and graph.json gives `ambient` (`unit`, `as` and any STALE `reasons`). The mark changes no drawing: the overview classifies its common modules from the structure (below), and graph.json gives the marks as a reader wrote them.
- **Scope marks.** A box the record marks as a sample, an artifact or a tool (`scope`) shows how to use, build or test the product and is no part of it. The overview leaves it out of the picture (`aside.go`), so in the picture it takes no place among the product's boxes and is no part of their count, and the overview names it in a line under the picture, `주변: A · B`, each name linking the box's focus view and its title giving the scope. Its lines are counted and listed under the picture like any left out, and its focus view draws them all. A stage of the primary flow, and a box a chosen line of the record or the lead runs through, stays a box, since a reader reads through those lines. Where the record marks no box, nothing changes. A marked box also takes no line from a box outside its scope for a contract it provides (`apart`). The mark's state is as an ambient mark's: stamped, STALE with its code, and a stale mark counts as none.
- **Common modules.** A box of the overview is common when at least 3 other boxes use it, they are at least half of the others, and the relations that run into it from other boxes are at least twice those that run out of it to other boxes (`common.go`). A box many use that uses as much back is coupled both ways, not common. A common module affects the logic when a DATA, CFG or EXEC unit inside it is reached by a unit of another box, when it provides a contract another box uses, or when it is a stage of the primary flow; otherwise it is a utility. A unit inside it that no other box reaches counts for nothing, so a setting a helper layer reads for itself leaves it a utility. Everything is read off the state, the same way on every repository, with no switch, no record and nobody to confirm it. A utility stands as a foundation band: out of the layout, under every other box and the gap under the last row, as wide as they span and at least `boxMax` wide, one band under another, left out of every drawn zone. None of its lines is drawn, in or out, and its last line says `기반 · N개 상자가 사용`, N the boxes that use it; its tooltip names every one of them, every box it uses, and the words each user's relations into it carry. Its pairs stay in `Counted`, so level L and every count read them, they stay in the list under the picture, and every box gets a focus view that draws all its lines, seated where the overview without the band sets it. A utility that is a stage of the primary flow, that a line of the current record ends on, or that the lead rides through stays a box, since a reader reads through those lines. graph.json says `band` of each common module the overview stands as a band. A common module that affects the logic keeps its box and its lines, and its tooltip says why. Below the drawing the overview names each common module with its kind and links `common.html`, the common modules' page: per module, the boxes that use it in a row over a band that stands for it, each user's words beside its line, the reasons for its kind, the words each user's relations carry, and the boxes that do not use it, which is what the band loses at rest; and the third-party libraries two boxes or more share, with those boxes (a library one box uses stays in that box's focus view). The page exists only when there is a common module or a shared library. graph.json gives `common` (`unit`, `name`, `kind` `logic` or `utility`, `reasons`, `users`, `non_users`, `in`, `out`, `phrases`, `band`) and `libraries` (`unit`, `name`, `users`). A band takes its lines off the page and any word they carry at rest, so where the overview with its bands has fewer lines that carry a word than without them, every utility stays a box, and the page says why. Over the 15 audit sets, the fixtures and four held-out repositories the band changes 3 overviews (fastify, gin, lua), none worse on any measure: summed over the 20, crossings go from 14 to 13, the hard breaches from 2 to 1, the weighed sum from 353 to 322, the bends from 402 to 392, the elements from 1098 to 1083 and the width from 20318 to 20200, while the routes (260) and the lines that carry a word (127) stay. The pstack fixture's two utilities stay boxes, since their bands would take two words off its overview.
- **Read by tools.** graph.json gives `primary` from the stack marked primary (its `Key`, `Question` and `Stages`, each with `N`, `Name` and `Units`), and each unit on it ranks by its stage (`Rank`, O4). It gives `zones` (`name`, `units` and any STALE `reasons`), `ambient` (`unit`, `as` and any STALE `reasons`), `common` and `libraries` (above) and `lines` (`from`, `to`, `word`, `kinds`, `vias`, `relations` and any STALE `reasons`). Each unit's row gives the `Zone` name that holds it, itself or through a unit that holds it. `abbd explain ID` prints the stage of the primary flow and the zone that hold the unit, in Korean when the project's language is Korean (`주 흐름 charge 2/5단계 billing`, `구역 돈`) and in English otherwise. A box no stage names, whose inside a stage names, stands at the first such stage, as the overview draws it, and explain names the unit inside it (`주 흐름 charge 2/5단계 billing, 안의 DATA-0001로`).
- **Writing the record.** `abbd overview` with no FILE prints the stored zones and lines, or empty ones, under YAML comments that give what the state offers a writer. They are the boxes the overview draws, each with the stage it stands at, the primary flow's stages, zone candidates, and line candidates. The zone candidates are the groupings the overview's bands draw, read from the state: each layer of a stack not marked primary that holds two boxes or more, and each folder that holds two boxes or more but not all of them. The line candidates are every pair of boxes a live relation joins in its direction, a use of a contract drawn as a chip counted to each of its providers. They are ranked by what the relations under them carry: `runs` first (a `posts_to`, a `call`, `data`, a `trait` or `callback`, or no via), then `wires` (`config`, `convention`, `load`, `spawn` and the other build or design ties), then `imports`. Within a rank, the pair with more relations of that rank comes first, then the one with more relations in all. Each candidate is written as a line ready to keep, its `word` empty and its `evidence` the first quote of its strongest relation that has one, with a comment giving its relations by kind and via. When no relation under it quotes code, as a build model's edges do not, its `evidence` is a line of the from box's files that names what a relation runs to: an entry of the contract the relation uses, a symbol the unit anchors, its name spelt as an identifier, or a file it anchors, a call before a bare mention, held once in its file and in the code of an end. The comment says the quote was found by name, and a candidate with no such line says so and where to look. The whole printout is the file, its frontmatter between `---` lines, so it stores as printed. abbd reads past the comments, so the writer keeps the candidates a person needs, words each from a quoted line and stores the file. The abbd-setup skill says how to choose.

## Pages

Every sync rewrites the blueprint from the state, and `.abbd/` is committed with the code, the pages and `graph.json` included, so a fresh clone has them. The repository's `.gitattributes` marks `.abbd/blueprint/**` as `linguist-generated`, so a pull request's diff collapses the pages and a reviewer reads the state they are drawn from. A merge conflict in a page is resolved by taking either side and running `abbd sync`, which draws the pages again from the merged state. A checkout that lacks the pages reports them NOT DRAWN, and `abbd sync` draws them. `abbd diff --page` draws the base's pages from the state committed at the base, so both sides read in the renderer of now. The views are split across pages by their level, so that a change rewrites only the pages of the views that draw it (`site.go`).

- **`index.html` holds only the overview.** It draws the modules and the contracts between them, never the third-party libraries they use, and lists the flows, stacks and tables by their questions and sizes. Beside each figure it shows only counts: its STALE parts, its breaches, and its unbacked and unsaid cells. It is the one page whose nav lists every module.
- **A unit drawn on the overview has a page, `<ID>.html`,** when it holds units or has a focus view on the overview. Its inside's drawing takes the section's whole width. The module's description and the list of its units sit in a panel right below the drawing, its legend and its picker, side by side where the page is wide enough and one above the other where it is not. The unwired contracts, the flows, stacks and tables the module is in, the inferred couplings with their quotes, and the contracts' entries come after that panel. The page shows its inside first, then its focus view on the overview, then the focus views of its inside. A unit with a focus view and no inside has a page with only that view.
- **Each flow, stack and table has a page of its own.** It marks the figure's STALE parts, breaches and unbacked or unsaid cells, each with why. The overview's list shows only their counts. So a figure going STALE rewrites its page and one line of the overview. A change to a STALE part's reasons or words rewrites only the figure's page, since no count moves.
- **Every other page's nav** links the overview and both indexes. A unit's page lists the modules it sits in, down to its own unit. A figure's page belongs to no module, so its crumb names the figure and its nav lists no module. No page but the overview shows counts or siblings. So a unit that comes or goes rewrites the pages of the views that draw it and the indexes, and the overview's nav only when the unit has a page, never every page.
- **`graph.json` holds the data and, of the drawing, only the order it is read in.** It lists the units, every live relation, each module's inside less its drawing, and the figures. Each unit a view draws as a box carries its `Rank` there, a unit on the overview in `units` and a unit inside a module in that module's `Units`, so an agent reads the boxes in the order a person sees them. `Basis` names the axis the view reads by, top to bottom. On `dependency` the layout layers the view's lines, each user above what it uses where no cycle stands between them. On `stack` the overview's rows follow a stored stack: each box the stack holds stands on its layer, from the top, a breach of the stack included, and every other box stands by the lines. `N` is the row the box is drawn in, 1 at the top, of `Of` rows. A row is a band of boxes whose heights overlap, as lint reads it. A layer wider than the view wraps into rows, so two boxes of one layer may stand on different rows, and `N` orders them as drawn, not as layered. `Cycle` marks a box that shares a cycle of the lines the layout layered with another box of the view. On the overview those are the `depends_on` lines and the lines from a contract's user to its provider, each box counting the relations of every unit it holds, and on an inside the lines between its units. They are read from the very graph the layout ranked, so the two cannot drift. No order runs between such boxes, so on `dependency` their rows are the layout's choice and carry no meaning. A change that moves boxes or lines within their rows rewrites the pages that draw them and leaves `graph.json` as it was. A change that moves a box to another row rewrites the ranks too.
- **A quote is cited by its file and its words.** The pages and `graph.json` leave out the quote's lines, which an edit above it moves while the quote still says the same. The store and `abbd evidence` keep the lines.
- **A crossing mask is named by its own line** (rule 24), so a change to one line renames no other line's mask.

The levels of abstraction stay as they were. The overview draws every module and every relation that crosses a module's boundary, an inside draws its units, and a focus view one unit and its ring. 453 jogs, 313 crossings and 69 phantom pairs.

## What the overview draws

Every view is deterministic, so the same state renders as identical bytes. Every view, the overview included, draws every live relation whatever its origin. A reader's coupling (판독) carries its hollow dot there too, because sync re-checks its quote on every run. A dead coupling, whose quote is gone, is drawn nowhere.

So a relation from anywhere inside a top-level unit to another top-level unit is drawn on the overview as that unit's own: it crosses the boundary the overview shows. A unit inside a module is never drawn on the overview, and a change that stays inside a module never moves it.

A third-party library is one `EXT` unit that `abbd libs` reads from a `go.mod`, `package.json` or `Cargo.toml`, anchored at the manifest that declares it by the library's name. The standard library and the system's headers are never one. The overview draws no library: no chip, no line to one and no fold chip, so what it shows is the product's own boxes. The overview record may mark a library a participant, one the primary flow crosses or that carries out a stage; the mark is recorded and checked for staleness, and a mark that stands is drawn as a dotted box in a row titled 저장소 밖 above the picture, where the flow starts (`participants.go`, `outsideOf`). That box is no box of the overview: it joins no layout, no line and no count of boxes, and the picture itself is unchanged. Its hover and the words beside its name say which stages of the primary flow reach it. Where no participant is marked, nothing changes. Each library stays reachable below it. A module that uses a library has a focus view even where the overview is sparse and draws none for the rest. A focus view keeps its module's libraries, each as a chip of its own, apart from the product's boxes and never as a box, with one plain line from the module and never a line per call; the chip's hover says how many boxes use it. Past a quarter of the focus view's boxes, and past one, they fold into one `외부 라이브러리 N개` chip whose line's hover names the libraries it uses and which carries each stored relation. A line to a library takes no phrase and never shares a road with a line to a box. Two library chips or more sit in a band of their own, `외부 라이브러리`. An inside view draws none, `units.html` names on each library's row every unit that uses it, and the graph, `abbd libs`, `abbd explain`, `abbd path` and `abbd impact` keep every library and its relations. A library chip counts among its view's boxes (rule 1). The library is told apart by its kind, so no reader writes one; a reader's mark only says which of the libraries is a participant.

## Grouping levels

A level of the tree is the one the project shows. A merge is stored only where an engineer would draw it: a directory that exists in the repository, a package or workspace, a skill family, a subsystem the project's own docs name, or an abstraction the code itself shows, such as a layer or the implementers of one interface. A merge made to make a view smaller or a dense view easier to draw goes past that, and makes the system harder to understand. The blueprint splits its views into the whole (the overview), a part (a module's inside) and one unit (a focus view), and a click on a box follows that module inside. That is how a crowded or dense level is read. The grouping is stored as units (MOD) and `parent` links, and the renderer never invents one at render time.

- **The extraction or a reader names it.** The extraction script reports the parents the code shows. The supplement step of `abbd-setup` adds only a grouping the project itself shows, as a MOD whose `evidence` quotes the project naming or showing it, and gives each member that part as its `parent`, with `parent_evidence`. `abbd record parent ID PARENT --evidence ...` places a single unit later, and `--source` records a human's statement instead. Such a parent is INFERRED or USER_STATED, and sync keeps it unless the extraction script reports a parent for that unit. A reader's parent lasts only as long as its evidence. A parent that would close a cycle is refused.
- **Sync's directories.** When a crowded level's units were placed by the script or by nobody, `abbd sync` places them in the directories their code lives in, at the shallowest depth that splits them. Inside a module it groups only the units below the module's own directory, the deepest one that holds all its anchors: a group of that directory or one above it would repeat the module, and a unit outside it stays in the module as it is. An anchor counts as a directory, and holds itself, when an anchor lies under it, so a module anchored at `src/core` spans `src/core` and not `src`. It stores each directory as a MOD unit whose anchor is that directory with the symbol `(group)`. It never moves a unit a reader or a human placed. It recomputes them on every sync and reuses IDs by directory.
