## Overview

`internal/screen` turns the `.abbd/` store into the blueprint a human reads. `Load` reads the store, `Build` derives a `Model`, and `Files` renders the blueprint's files. The pages are static HTML with no script, cut from the one model: `index.html` holds only the overview, each module or feature that holds units or has a focus view on the overview has its own `<ID>.html`, each flow, stack and table has its own page, and `contracts.html` and `units.html` index every contract and every unit. `graph.json` holds the unit rows, every live relation, the module lists without their drawings and the figures, for tools. So a change rewrites the pages of the views that draw it, and a change inside one module rewrites that module's page and `graph.json`. The pages and `graph.json` cite a quote by its file and words through `cite`, without its lines, so moving a quote's lines alone rewrites no page. A change to the lines around a quote marks its claim STALE, and that mark lands on the pages that show the claim. The CLI's `publish` (`cmd/abbd/screen.go`) runs after every command marked `writes` (`setup`, `sync`, `record relation|unit|parent|fact|evidence`, `retract`, `migrate`, `understand`, `forget`, `flow`, `stack`, `table`). It renders nothing for an empty store. Once every file is written and any other file under `blueprint/` removed, it records in the state's `Blueprint` the inputs hash it rendered from and in `Rendered` the hash of the files it wrote, so no file holds any value of the sync. `abbd status` reuses `Load`, through the same `screenInput`, for its freshness verdicts, and the page points at `abbd status` instead of stating one. The package imports `internal/model`, `internal/store`, `internal/topology` and `internal/narrative`, never writes the topology, and draws no box the store lacks, apart from a focus view's twin.

The overview draws each top-level module and feature as a box, and a top-level contract no module provides as a loose box. A `contract` line runs from a user's chip on the bottom edge to the provider's chip on the top edge. One chip stands for the contracts that line carries, named when it is one and counted when it is more, and the contracts no line reaches fold into one `+N 계약` chip on the provider. A thick `bypass` line with an open head marks a module dependency no contract line already carries. Two modules that depend on each other that way share one `mutual` line with a head at each end. A relation from a unit at any depth is lifted to the top-level unit that contains it. INFERRED couplings are drawn too, because sync rechecks their quotes. Only dead ones are dropped, and a drifted one is drawn like a live one. A drawn edge counts as extracted when any relation under it is the script's, else stated, else inferred. A small circle at a line's start marks a reader's or a human's relation, a bundle of both is `mixed`, and a dashed line runs against the reading direction. Every top-level module and every unit that holds others has its own view, and a box links to one only when `viewOf` names it, so no link opens a missing page. A feature with no parts has none. Everything is sorted before it is placed, so the same store always draws the same picture. `docs/blueprint-svg.md` states the drawing rules. `legible.go` measures them per line and channel, `trace.go` traces every relation along the drawn ink and counts the bends and the short jogs on its path, and `lint.go` holds every drawing to the Drawing criteria on top of both.

## Key Concepts

- **`Input` / `Model`** (`screen.go`): `Input` is what `Load` reads, including each module's understanding and its code digest now. `Load` leaves `Tree`, the git snapshot, for the caller to fill. `Model` is what `Build` derives. It holds the unit catalogue, the `Overview`, `Links` between two contracts listed as text, one `Inside` per view, the overview's `Focus` views, every live relation as `[]Line` for `graph.json` (`graph.go`), and the extraction command the page footer names.
- **`tree`** (`tree.go`): the parent tree the views follow. A unit whose parent is absent moves to the top level, so nothing present drops out. A unit with children and no live relations of its own is abstract, and an inside box for it draws its members' relations (`sources`). `within` and `outside` say which child of a view, or which largest unit outside it, stands for an end.
- **`overview`** (`overview.go`): the plan `planOverview` builds and the insides reuse. `top` lists the top-level units and the live relations between them, with the stored relations and origins each drawn edge stands for. Each top-level contract gets its `providers` and `users`, and a module that provides a contract is not drawn as its user.
- **`Drawing` / `Box` / `Port` / `Wire`** (`overview.go`): one SVG picture with its coordinates set. A `Box` is a top-level module or feature, an inside unit, or a loose contract. Its `Href` opens a view the blueprint holds, and it stays empty when there is none. On the overview, `chipFor` makes one chip per set of contracts a drawn line carries, labelled with its one contract or with `계약 N개` joined by a no-break space so `shorten` keeps the word. A module whose chips need more than `chipWrap` wraps them into at most two rows instead of widening, and the last row folds what does not fit into one "+N" `Port`. `settle` then moves a single unfolded row of chips over the x their lines run at. A chip is at most `chipMax` wide: `chipLines` wraps a longer label onto more lines, broken after a separator or before a space, so it is never cut and never leaves its edge, and every chip in a row takes the row's height, which `chipBand` sums for a box or a frame edge. On an inside frame, two ports that land on one spot share a chip, and its `Keys` name every port it stands for. `Drawing.Relations` lists the pairs of places the view requested before routing, which the checker holds the ink to. A line's ink is drawn in pieces, its stub, the buses it rides and their drops, so `route` also gives each request a `Route` along its whole way (`routing.ways`), drawn unseen above the ink in its source's colour and taking no pointer, with arrowheads that are `<use>` of heads painted in the route's own colour. A route turns where its line's ink turns, so a line that rides a bus past its drop and back shows that detour lit too, and `TestFewLinesDoubleBack` holds such lines to a count that may only fall. `hover` numbers a drawing's boxes and chips, a twin with its unit and a contract chip with its box, classes each route and dot by the shapes at its ends, each piece of ink by every route that runs along it, and each shape by the shapes its relations join it to. The fixed `hoverCSS` rules, with no script on the page, fade all the ink while a reader points at ink or a shape and show the lines lit as their routes, whole: pointed-at ink shows every route along it in its own colour, with its marks and its ends, and keeps its title, and a pointed-at box shows exactly its one-hop lines in the box's colour (`--kN`, set by `paint`). A drawing with more than `hoverMax` shapes or `routeMax` routes takes no part, and only a page with a drawing carries the rules. A climbing line is drawn in a lighter tint of its colour, and `faint` lightens the dots only climbing lines meet at.
- **`Lead` / `Zone` / dress** (`lead.go`, `zone.go`, `dress.go`): what a view adds to read like a hand-polished diagram, after `paint`. `lead` picks the view's lead: the stored flow whose steps run furthest on the view, each next step on a box a drawn line joins to the lead, or, with no flow stored, the line chain from the one box no other box uses, along the line with strictly the most relations, stopping at a tie. Fewer than two hops draws none. Each hop is a route the view already draws, numbered with a badge off boxes, labels and counts, and `quiet` greys every other line, head and dot while routes keep their colours for hover. `zone` tints the boxes of a stored stack's layers, else of a shared directory (`home`), plus the outside contracts, even a single one, only where their padded area holds no other box, chip or band; its title takes the corner fewest lines cross. `dress` dots a line whose relations all arise by convention, import, trait, macro or build feature, fills a hub (half again as many lines as any other box) where no lead fills its entry, and gives each box a small-caps kind and role line where its chips leave room. A hop's number is set by `number` after dress, on ink no other hop runs along where it can, keeping `inkGap` from all ink not its own (`badgeClear`); a band's title keeps the corner it always took when that keeps clear of ink, labels and counts (`clearOfInk`), else the first clear place in its room, the other room, or a taller room on two lines. `card` keeps a small key (`Card`, titled 범례) in a free corner of a view, or along an edge within a third of one, a sample of each mark the view draws as the sample shows it, clear of every box, line, word, number and band (`room`). `paint` gives a bypass the colour of the box it leaves like any other line, its open head marking it, and a hub's view greys the other lines only where the hub's own are at least half its ink (`ownLines`). A quiet view drops a label that now stands over a flat track of another line of its colour (`unlabelKin`). A view past hover's caps is never made quiet, and chips on a filled box carry `OnFill` for a panel outline. The overview key reads `Quiet` through `legendFor`, so a quiet view's key draws the bypass grey. `Box.Role` carries entry or hub apart from `Class`, which the level check reads. A hub's view is quiet too, but `hubLines` keeps in the hub's colour the ink whose every route has the hub at one end. `dress` gives each chip on a box that box's colour (`Port.Hue`): `page.css` tints an exit chip 15% toward it, writing it in that colour drawn 20% toward the ink, and outlines an entry chip in it, and a chip on a filled box keeps a panel-coloured border. `zoneWidth` measures a title as the 10px monospace capitals 0.14em apart that the page sets. `page.css` draws each coloured box with a 1.5px border in its colour over a 5% tint (`tintMix`), with no bar, and thickens an inner box that has an inside to 2.6px under the pointer. It draws a pointed-at or focused box's border in its own colour (`--c`), the colour hover lights its lines in, and darkens a filled box instead; a focus view's centre box keeps its weight. It also draws a bypass at the 1.5px of any other line, so its orange and open head mark it without the weight.
- **`arrange` / `layered`** (`layout.go`, `layered.go`): the layered layout. It orders nodes to leave few edges pointing back (Eades, Lin and Smyth), ranks without those edges, and sets each node above what it uses. A node nothing uses sits just above the highest thing it uses, and nodes with no line gather on a bottom layer. Each pair of places more than a row apart gets a `chain` of dummies. `order` sorts every row, chains included, by one `tactic`, then sets a box joined to nothing last in its row. `Build` lays each view of a unit out with every tactic, and `choose` keeps a later tactic's drawing only where its `cost` beats the best so far, which starts as the first tactic's. The overview keeps the plain order of its rows, since every module's page holds a focus view seated by it, but `choose` picks its ranking from `overviewTactics`: plain, `fold`, which leaves the edges of two places that use each other out of the ranking, as one two-headed line joins them, and `entry`, which breaks each cycle at the node the places already ranked use most. `arrange` marks a fold or entry that changes nothing `idle`, and the draw closure skips it. For the overview `cost.over` is how far the drawing runs past one screen (criterion S): `screenW` wide and the view's `room` tall, `screenH` less what its page sets above the drawing, the overview's questions (`overviewRoom`) or a page's note. Whether it runs past is weighed right after the hard breaches and how far only after lint's weighed sum. A fold or entry drawing is passed over when it moves more boxes than the least plainly ranked one, or when it neither has fewer hard breaches nor fits where the plain one does not, so a small change cannot flip the overview's ranking to save a few turns; `fold_test.go` holds the fold and `fit_test.go` the choice. `judge` lints each drawing of a unit's view as `abbd lint` does, except one that already loses to the best so far on the touches and breaks of its routing, which `beats` compares first. `cost.beats` compares touches, then breaks of rules 13 and 15, then lint's hard breaches, then the overflow, then the crossings plus the excess turns plus `stepCrossings` times the staircase steps lint traces on the page, then lint's weighed sum, both with `stepCrossings` more for each placement breach and each chip folded into a "+N" chip, which its `Port` marks `Folded`, so a line that turns twice in a clear column, 2 excess turns and no step, cannot buy crossings, then `stepCrossings` times the staircase steps plus the crossings, the steps counted once per pair of places along each line's way from the `routing` that `route` returns, then the steps alone, paths that turn more than twice, braid and dashed ink, and the first measure that differs decides. A step is a turn past a line's second or a jog, and it weighs as much as 4 crossings. A `seated` tactic also sets a unit's wired frame chips as `chipNode` rows above and below the boxes, ordered and placed with them, so each chip stands over the chain its line takes and keeps that x as `settle`'s target. A view whose chips do not fit their sides, a focus view and the overview are not drawn seated or laned, since a chip handed to the other side would leave the seat or lane it was set over behind, and the draw closure says so. `lane_flip_test.go` holds a one-box view and a three-row view to that guard, and shows the three-row view takes lanes once its chips fit. A `laned` tactic instead gives each wired frame chip whose lines pass rows a lane, one chain from the frame's edge down every row its lines pass, joined in each row to the boxes it meets in the next (`proper`); `place` pulls the lane toward the nearest box it reaches, `route` runs the chip's lines down it, and the chip stands over its first row. With `follow`, `order` then moves each lane among the other items of its row to where what it joins in the next row stands, rows bottom up for top lanes and top down for bottom ones, so a lane runs beside its box. A `loose` tactic leaves the chips to be set after the layout but has both sweeps of `order` place every dummy of a chain from the frame's edge by its box end alone: otherwise a sweep down sets each under its dummy in the row above, so chains from the frame keep their first order all the way down, and one that ends on a box left of the others runs right of them and turns across them into it. The draw closure skips it for a view with no wired frame chip. A `cover` tactic, plain or loose, marks a chip `one` when every line of it runs down a chain from the frame's edge that has lanes for more than one line, in a view that is not a focus view; `settle` then stands such a chip off centre, back to where its line still meets its edge 4px in, when the chip before it would push it off its line, and the chips before it give way while their own lines still meet them. Two chips over one box whose lines share a chain 16px apart then stand edge to edge over their lanes, and both lines drop straight. The draw closure skips it for a view with no such chip. A `reorder` tactic, loose and cover, then lets `reorder` move chips of each frame edge to other places in their order while fewer chips stand over none of the x their lines run at (`attach.runs`), then less distance from them: on a crowded edge the order of the mean x pushed a chip with one line far off it while a chip whose lines spread wide stood where that line runs. It is drawn only where it moves a chip, and `reorder_test.go` holds src/'s bottom edge to it. A `gather` tactic, plain and median, draws one chip per box and edge for the modules outside that meet that box with no contract (a port keyed by `gatherPort` and the box, a key no title, label or link shows), named while the names fit `gatheredPx` and the label fits one line, cut only between names and counted as `+N` past that, so each box takes one line from the frame. Each gathered line keeps the tooltip line and evidence of every line it gathers, said with its real far end, and a chip that gathers one module links to that module's view. It is drawn only where some outside module meets two or more boxes, `choose` keeps it where it crosses no more than the least of the ungathered drawings, or else no more than the drawing it replaces while adding no line that turns in a clear column, and a gathered view's focus views gather the same way (`Inside.gathered`); `gather_test.go` holds it. `choose` passes over a laned drawing whose `cost.moved`, its placement breaches and folded chips, exceeds that of the best drawing with no lanes by more than `laneMoves` (1), or whose weighed sum passes that drawing's, and `admit` runs every check before it lowers a bound, so a drawing it passes over sets none: lanes save steps by standing chips off their boxes and trade excess turns for steps, which `seen` weighs less than the gate does. Each ordering is drawn twice: wide, where `place` lets every block spread over the whole budget toward what it joins and then trims the drawing to what the blocks cover, and `narrow`, packed as tight as the order lets it, at `narrowBudget` for a unit's view. The draw closure leaves each drawing's cost in the `read` it is handed, and a focus view, handed none, is never weighed. `wrap` splits a layer wider than the budget into rows. Where the boxes with a line to what lies below fit in a wrapped layer's last row, `stack` puts them there, so their lines leave from the bottom row. `shed` takes the shortest chains out while the rows are still too wide with every chain crooked. `place` treats each box and each straight run of a chain as one block, Brandes and Köpf style. Each chain, longest first, becomes as few runs as it can: a run grows down while the order holds and the drawing fits its budget, found by halving. `shorten` then moves an item along its row, past up to `moveMost` items, where that saves more than `crossCost` of line on the placed drawing, a crossing counted as `crossCost`, and the rows are placed again unless that takes the drawing past its budget. A focus view passes a `focusSeat`, so `seat` orders rows by the full view's x, `brick` deals a wide ring into interleaved rows, and `ring` packs each row around the focus on one vertical axis.
- **`route`** (`route.go`): orthogonal routing on that grid. A line crosses each gap on its own track and passes rows down its chain or between boxes. `straighten` moves a chain that passes its rows outside the span its lines' ends share onto the middle of that span, where every row it passes has room, and `route` keeps that drawing only where `weigh` finds it beats the other. Where it does not, `route` lays it once more with every crossing held at or under the track it took in the first drawing (`shelve`'s floor, keyed by request or by a bus's first member and side), so the track a straightened line left stays and the steps below keep their height. A line with no chain keeps the x it passed the row above at where the row lets it, and `gutterTo` steps it aside toward its end. A line whose x is the lane its own chain holds in a row keeps straight on it past a wide gap, when it stands 12px off the boxes, no other chain runs in that channel and no stub or other pass comes within a lane, so it does not swing out to the gap's middle and back. A full view drops a line between rows next to each other whose boxes' spans meet straight as well, outside hubs and shared buses, and keeps that drawing only where `weigh` finds it beats the bundled one. A line alone in its slot that passes no row and runs down no chain, between boxes in rows next to each other or between a frame chip and a first- or last-row box, takes the x its other end already stands at when that edge has it clear, so it drops straight. The arrival trunk a line leads may pass a row where that line's own departure trunk ended, so it runs on down the lead's chain instead of swinging out to a gap's middle. Otherwise they step down above the lead line's end, or at the clear x nearest it on the trunk's side, so no line rides past its drop and comes back. - **`junctions` / `anchor`** (`ink.go`): what the routed paths look like to a reader. `junctions` puts a `Dot` wherever ink of one colour goes three or more ways, and where a line crosses onto a trunk of its own kind that runs past it. `anchor` sets each ×N beside its own stub or drop: first where it stands clear of other lines, then where it keeps off every arrowhead (`heads`, read from the drawn paths), then where the nearest other ink is farthest. `route` anchors only the routing it keeps, since `weigh` and `costOf` never read the counts.
- **`mark` / `paint` / `gaps` / `labels`** (`marks.go`, `hues.go`): what the page adds to the routed ink after the dots and counts. `mark` records the place each line's relations leave, cuts the gaps and sets the labels. After every view is drawn, `Build` calls `colours` once over all of `Model.drawings`, so a unit wears one colour in every view. Two units are joined when some view draws a line between their boxes, a contract's chip included, and `colours` colours the most constrained unit first, picking the colour its joined units hold least. `paint` then gives each box its colour, drawn as a border in its colour over a faint tint of it, draws each line in the colour of the boxes its lines leave, or a neutral grey when they differ, and gives it an arrowhead whose shape says its kind. A direct dependency that skips the contract boundary wears the reserved warning orange instead, whatever box it leaves. `palette` is the one table of six colours, and `hueCSS` writes it, neutral and the warning into the page for both themes. `TestEveryHueReadsOnBothThemes` holds each at 3:1 or better, `TestTheWarningStandsApart` keeps every colour 30 or more from the warning under normal vision, protanopia and deuteranopia, and `TestJoinedBoxesWearDifferentColours` holds joined boxes to different colours. `gaps` cuts a horizontal piece wherever a vertical piece of another line crosses it away from both pieces' turns and ends, unless a dot of the kind both lines share joins them, as a `Gap` in an SVG mask on that line's path, whose id hashes only that line and its holes, so a change to one line renames no other mask. Crossings closer than `gapJoin` on one piece share one cut, so no sliver is left. The path keeps its points, so the checker's ink graph is unchanged. `labels` then sets the word `word` gives a line upright in 11px text beside one straight stretch of its own ink, horizontal stretches first, clear of boxes, chips, counts, dots, origin marks, arrowheads, other labels and other lines. A check names the boxes, lines and chips of each breach as `Item`s, by id and name, with what it measures against what is allowed, and for a line that turns too often the obstacle it turns around or the reason. `allowance` reads a line's allowed turns from where its two ends stand and the shapes around them, never from the route. `lines` pairs each traced line with its turns and allowance, and the per-view `Totals` add its turns past the allowance and every jog as the excess turns, the measure a change to the renderer lowers. `tees` finds each tee, a line's straight drop that crosses a flat stretch of another line from the same place away from any dot, which a reader takes for a branch: it counts as a crossing and weighs `teeCrossings`, more than two turns. The weighed sum is the crossings, `teeCrossings` less one more for each tee, plus `stepCrossings` for each excess turn, and the placement debt counts the breaches of the placement criteria. `lay` refuses a straight drop that would make a tee, in focus views too, so the line takes its bus, and it draws the own parts of lines that share one path, kind, joins and back as one path whose title names every line, so no line hides another from the pointer. `forks` lets a branch of a fork turn twice: a line between boxes in rows next to each other that leaves its box's bottom down a stem another of the box's lines of its kind drops straight down, every line on it of that kind, ending in that row and turning onto that same flat track, and no other place's line along its stretch of the track. `noTurnBacks` (B3) finds a line whose stretch runs against the stretch two before it (`turnsBack`), counts a turn-back that several lines share once, and leaves out a back line (`backWay`), which rule 13 counts. `noDoubles` (B4) finds two stretches of one traced line, or of two subpaths of one bundle, that run the same way side by side within `doubleGap` (`sideBySide`), and skips a bundle's pair along a pair a traced line already counts (`onStretch`). Rule 4's split counts in `measure` group the lines at a box edge by `kindOf` their class, not by the hover classes each line carries, and leave out a line that drops straight from box to box. `Sum` adds the per-view totals. `gate.go` holds views to a `store.LintDebt`: `Gate` marks the debt and lists the failures, `Lowerable` what the debt records above what is measured, and `Record` the debt of what is measured now, each ceiling at the lower of its total and the ceiling the old debt sets, so a ceiling never rises on its own. The `gated` totals are the weighed sum, the placement debt, which may pass its ceiling by 2% of it, the overflow, the views past one screen (S), and the detours, none of which may pass theirs. `lint_test.go` holds each fixture to its own `.abbd/lint-debt.yaml` with `Gate` and `Lowerable`, and with `ABBD_LINT_FIXTURES` set writes the per-view table that `eval/lint/compare.py` reads. `countAmbiguity` (`trace.go`) holds each ×N to rule 22: within reach of one stretch, clear of the next nearest, and nearer the ink of its own wire than any other wire's. `readSVG` takes a count's wire to be the net of the path just before it, every path that shares its `data-net`, when only that path's title and origin marks stand between them, as the template writes them; a count set apart from its paths, as in a hand-laid drawing, has no wire and keeps the stretch test alone.
